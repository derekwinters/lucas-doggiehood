    World Art¶

    Epic: #795. Spec: #800. Decisions: #796, #797

    The world around the houses is being upgraded along with them: textured lawns, roads and sidewalks, textured trees, a small set of street props, and fences. Everything follows the art direction: clean, stylized 3D with realistic colours and real textures, where textures carry the detail rather than polygon count. Houses have their own page, House Design.

    This is the contract, not yet the code

    The game still renders the flat grass colour, the Kenney roads, sidewalks, fences and yard trees described in World & Neighborhood and Sidewalks & Walk Network until the implementation issues land (#808–#813). No world implementation starts before Derek approves every world model on #807.

    How the models are made¶

    Every world model is a Blender Python script under art-source/blender/, built by Claude and reviewed by Derek, using the same Blender asset pipeline as the houses (conventions Open on #774 and #775). Every model stays within the art budget.

    Textures are shared, not copied. Each textured set exports as .gltf + .bin with its images referenced from one shared textures/ folder, so each texture is stored once. The approved trees (#816) and street props (#821) are built this way under art-source/blender/environment/.

    Reference-only assets. Some approved models are kept as source and reference designs for a consistent style, but are not used in the game (#797). They stay outside Assets/.

    Invariant — reference-only assets never ship in the build. Only models this page lists as in use are imported under Assets/. A reference-only model is promoted by changing this page first, not by importing it.

    Invariant — a world model's footprint matches the tile or lot geometry it replaces exactly. A new road, sidewalk, walkway or fence model keeps today's widths, offsets and positions, so placement code, walk paths and truck paths do not change.

    Ground: lawns¶

    Every lot gets a textured lawn, a tileable grass texture rather than today's flat grass colour.

    Open (#801, #808): the lawn texture itself (colour, scale in metres, how visible repeats are hidden); whether the grass verge and the open-space fill use the same lawn; and how lot edges meet sidewalks and fences.

    Later: mowing lawns as a chore, with grass that looks lightly overgrown when due, is an idea for the future and not part of this page (#820).

    Roads¶

    The Kenney road tiles are replaced by a Blender road kit covering every road tile type in the Tile Catalog. It keeps today's road width (6 m), the sidewalk offsets and the crosswalk positions exactly.

    Approved look (#802): look A, "Fresh blacktop", for all 15 road tile types, source in art-source/blender/environment/roads/. The approved street-prop road decals (crosswalk, stop line, manhole cover, storm drain) are reference only.

    Road layout rules (#837):

    • Stops. One Core rule decides which intersection approaches stop: at a tee, only the stem (the side street) stops; at a four-way, every approach stops (an all-way stop). Stop lines and stop signs are both placed from that rule, so a stop line always has a sign and a sign always has a stop line.
    • Cul-de-sacs keep today's rounded dead end. There is no wide paved turning circle, so the walk network is unchanged.
    • Intersection corners stay square.
    • Paved landings: crosswalk landings and box corners are paved across the 0.75 m grass verge, so a dog never crosses grass to reach a crosswalk.

    Invariant — a stop line is drawn only where a stop sign stands, and every stop sign has its stop line.

    How the spec is changing (#837). The road look was Open, stop signs went on "each side-street approach" with the streets undecided, and the road kit drew a stop line on every approach → the road look is approved, a tee stops only its stem and a four-way is an all-way stop, and stop lines follow the signs → because Derek agreed on 2026-11-26.

    Sidewalks, front walkways and driveways¶

    Sidewalks and front walkways keep every width and position in Sidewalks & Walk Network (sidewalk 2 m, grass verge 0.75 m).

    Driveways. A house with a garage gets a driveway: ground decoration in front of the garage only (#817). It adds no walk-network edge. Dogs still reach the front door by the front walkway, which stays the only way on or off a lot (#128).

    Open (#803): the concrete surfaces and joints, and the driveway's exact shape and size.

    Trees¶

    Trees use the approved textured technique (#816): each crown is a few hundred alpha-cut leaf cards carrying painted leaf sprigs, on a light branch skeleton with textured, normal-mapped bark. Detail comes from textures, not polygon count.

    The approved set is 10 trees: maple, cherry, spruce and columnar, each large and young, plus a street tree and a Japanese maple. Their sizes and triangle counts are in art-source/blender/environment/trees/README.md.

    • Yard trees keep today's placement rules in World & Neighborhood → Yard landscaping: procedural, seeded per lot, clear of the house, walkway, fence and each other. Only the models change.
    • No street trees (#797). The 0.75 m grass strip stays as it is.
    • What Unity needs (from the trees README): leaves on a double-sided, alpha-clip material; mipmaps that preserve alpha coverage; imported (not recalculated) normals; and textures shared across trees.

    Open (#804, #811): which of the 10 trees appear in yards, and how today's large and small yard-tree picks map onto them; whether the street-tree model is used at all; and fading leaf cards that turn edge-on to the camera.

    Landscaping is something dogs ask for¶

    Flower beds, shrubs, hedges and similar yard planting are not world dressing placed automatically (#797). Each one is a purchasable item a dog asks for through a quest, installed on its lot the way a fence is (#318).

    Open: the landscaping items, their prices and tiers, and where each sits on a lot. Derek decides these later with the renders in front of him (#797).

    Street props¶

    Derek approved 26 street props as a reference set (#821). Six are in use in the game (#797):

    Prop Model Placement
    Stop sign stop_sign One on each stopping approach (a tee's stem, every approach of a four-way; see Roads), on the right-hand corner. It is the only sign in the game.
    Lamp post (lantern) lamp_post_lantern In the grass strip at every intersection corner, plus one at each property line along the block
    Mailbox mailbox One per house, at the curb beside its front walkway
    Fire hydrant fire_hydrant One per block, at a corner in the grass strip
    Park bench bench_park Only at a few corners (for example one corner per intersection), never in front of a house
    Newspaper box newspaper_box Only at a few corners (for example one corner per intersection), never in front of a house

    Sizes and triangle counts are in art-source/blender/environment/street-props/README.md.

    Reference only: every other sign (pedestrian crossing signs don't fit a world with only dogs in it), the cobra-head street light, the bus-stop bench, the trash and recycling carts, the dog-waste station, the bike rack, the utility pole and wires, and the road decals.

    Lamps don't glow. The lantern's glass shows no light until the game has a night (#797); lighting stays static daytime (Lighting & time). The source model's glass is emissive, so the in-game material turns that off.

    Invariant — a street prop never stands on the roadway and never blocks a sidewalk, walkway or front door (#797).

    Open (#805, #812): which corners get the bench and newspaper box; exact offsets inside the 0.75 m grass strip; and how the props follow map expansion onto new tiles.

    Fences¶

    The backyard fence keeps the geometry LotFence traces today (Backyard fences), built from modular Blender pieces.

    Open (#806, #813): the fence style or styles, and how a fence meets a house's upgrade pieces (#783).
