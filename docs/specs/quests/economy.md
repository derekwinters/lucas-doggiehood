# Quest & Economy

*Epic: [#15](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/15)*

## Core loop

The overall goal is helping dogs around the neighborhood by completing their requests, combined with a daily rotation of active quests. ([#23](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/23))

> **How the pacing is changing ([#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543), v0.11 playtest fix).** Quests used to arrive in one all-or-nothing batch of 2-4 every 8 hours. They now **trickle in hourly** instead: the same population-scaled target is spread over a **4-hour window** ([#624](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/624) shortened it from 6h), giving a per-hour rate of `target / 4`. A **persisted fractional accumulator** (error diffusion, Bresenham-style) handles rates that are not whole numbers — each hour it banks the fractional leftover and only ever adds *whole* quests, so a 1.25/hr floor neighborhood gets a quest most hours (and a second whenever the carried fraction tips over) and long-run it averages exactly `target / 4`. The **target itself, the ≥1-free-quest guarantee, and the headroom/free-dog cap are all unchanged** — only the cadence and how many arrive per refresh changed. (The "missed time is a single top-up" rule that #543 kept has since been reversed, and the cadence shortened again to 15 minutes — see [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743) below.)

- **Pacing**: a rotation trickles the neighborhood's active requests in, mixing quest types, a fraction of the target on every refresh (`target × interval / window`, i.e. a sixteenth of the target every 15 minutes) rather than a fixed batch. Keeps sessions short and gives a reason to return. ([#26](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/26), [#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543), [#624](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/624), [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743))
    - **Cadence — every 15 minutes after the board drops below target (UTC)**: the recurring refresh is owned by [#310](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/310) / [#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543), and since [#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704) the clock starts **when a slot opens** rather than running against a full board. The moment the active count falls below `TargetActiveCount` — a completed quest, or a move-in raising the target — the wait begins, stamped as a persisted **UTC timestamp** (`GameState.QuestRefreshTimerStartedUtc`); every `EconomyNumbers.RefreshInterval` (**15 minutes** since [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743), 1 hour before that) the trickle tops the board up, and the clock re-anchors if the board is still short or stops entirely once it is full. **No clock runs while the board is at target** — there is nothing to wait for. UTC — never the device-local clock — so changing the device timezone can neither double-fire nor stall the boundary. It is a boundary *check*, not a countdown/expiry: nothing is ever removed and no quest can fail (see **No pressure** below).
    - **Time away counts — every elapsed interval pays out ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743))**: returning after a break pays out **every** refresh interval that was due while the game was closed, not one. Away four hours is sixteen refreshes' worth; away four days is every interval in them. There is **no max-offline constant**, because the pacing window is already the ceiling: one window's worth of refreshes accrues exactly `TargetActiveCount`, so away 5 hours, 5 days or 5 months all land on the same full board and stop. The intervals are advanced in **one** accumulator step (`intervals × perRefresh`, floored once) rather than a loop — provably the same total as N separate steps, and the same cost for a four-month absence as a four-hour one — and the clock then re-anchors to the **last fully-consumed boundary** (`timerStart + intervals × interval`), never to *now*, so the partial interval the player returns in the middle of is neither swallowed nor paid twice. If the device clock has moved *backwards*, the elapsed interval count clamps at zero rather than subtracting.
    - **Time until the next *real* quest ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743))**: most refreshes add **zero** quests at a small target (11 of 16 at the floor), so a countdown pointing at the next *refresh* would hit zero four times an hour while a quest appeared once. `QuestPacingPolicy.TimeUntilNextQuest(state)` is the honest number — `ceil((1 − accumulator) / perRefresh)` intervals, or none at all on a full board — computed in Core from the persisted accumulator. The HUD readout that displays it is [#683](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/683); this rule ships only the number.
    - **Checked while you play, not only at launch ([#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704))**: the boundary used to be evaluated in exactly one place — the scene bootstrap — which made the refresh cadence effectively *per app launch*: a five-hour session produced no new quests at all, and the only mid-session path that added any was the Debug tab's "Refresh quests now". `QuestManager.TickQuests(nowUtc, rng)` is the recurring seam (the launch path, `EnsureQuestsForLaunch`, is the same decision plus onboarding's one-time seed); the Unity layer polls it on a short timer (`QuestDirector.PacingTickInterval`) and saves only when Core reports something changed. The onboarding gate is unchanged — the rotation stays suppressed until the guided reward chain completes.
    - **Per-refresh trickle — a fractional accumulator**: the number added on each refresh is `target × RefreshIntervalMinutes / (PacingWindowHours × 60)` — the population-scaled target spread over the pacing window (a named `EconomyNumbers` constant, **4 hours** since [#624](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/624)) and then sliced by the interval. At the shipping 15 min / 4h pair that is a sixteenth of the target per refresh: target 6 → 0.375 (1.5/hr), 12 → 0.75 (3/hr), the floor of 5 → 0.3125 (1.25/hr). Fractional rates are handled by a **persisted error-diffusion accumulator** (`GameState.QuestPacingAccumulator`, round-tripped by `SaveCodec` — a legacy save with no `questPacingAcc=` line loads at 0.0): each boundary it adds the rate, adds `floor(accumulator)` *whole* quests, and carries the remainder forward. A quest is never fractional, quiet refreshes that add 0 are expected and fine (11 of 16 at the floor target), and the long-run rate equals `target / 4` per hour. The per-refresh min/max fall out of this equation (min 0, max `ceil(perRefresh)`) rather than a fixed 2-4 batch.

        The interval is **granularity only** — it decides how *often* quests are added, never how many arrive in total. Read at each whole hour, the 15-minute cadence delivers exactly what the hourly one did (1·2·3·**5** at the floor, 1·3·5·**7** at target 7, 3·6·9·**12** at the ceiling); it just arrives in 16 small steps instead of 4 big ones, and a busy neighborhood sees its first quest at 30 or 45 minutes rather than always waiting a full hour. Where the interval does not divide the window evenly (a 3h interval in a 4h window, reachable only from the debug slider), **granularity may round the fill moment later, never the total higher** — the board fills at the next boundary instead of exactly at the window's close. That is the honest meaning of a coarse interval, so it is pinned by a test rather than special-cased: pro-rating a partial interval would pay quests out at a moment no refresh actually fires.
    - **Concurrent-quest cap — population-scaled**: a refresh is a **top-up toward** a target, never a blind add — it adds `min(wholeThisRefresh, target − activeCount, freeDogs)`, floored at 0 (where `wholeThisRefresh = floor(accumulator + intervals × perRefresh)`), so once the neighborhood already holds the target number of uncompleted quests a refresh adds nothing until the player clears some. **This is what bounds a catch-up too** — returning from a long absence fills the board *up to* the target and stops; it can never exceed it or double-book a dog. The leftover fraction is persisted regardless of this clamp, so a quiet stretch at the cap can never bank a flood. The target is `clamp(round(dogCount / 3), 5, 12)` — divisor, floor (5, raised from 3 in [#624](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/624)), and ceiling (12) are all named `EconomyNumbers` constants. Sample ramp: 8 dogs → 5, 12 → 5, 18 → 6, 24 → 8, 36 → 12, 100 → 12. The **ceiling (12) is the playtest flood-control dial** — drop it first if a mid-game map feels busy.
    - **Always one free quest — soft-lock protection**: to prevent a dead-end of 0 coins with every active quest requiring coins to accept, every refresh **that adds a quest** guarantees the post-refresh active set holds **≥1 free-type quest** (the no-cost types, LostItem / PestControl; the paid types are BuyGift / DecorationRequest). If a top-up would otherwise leave an all-paid set, one added quest is forced to a free type. This is enforced **only at the refresh boundary, never on quest completion** — instant replenish would be an unlimited-coin faucet. A temporary "no free quest" window is fine — including the quiet refreshes where the trickle adds nothing (completing anything frees a slot and the next refresh that adds a quest restores a free option); only *locked progress with no exit* is prevented.
    - **The pacing seam** (`QuestPacingPolicy`): the pacing decisions — `ShouldRefresh(nowUtc, state)` (has the wait elapsed) and `IsBoardBelowTarget(state)` (is a wait running at all — #704), `TargetActiveCount(state)` (cap), `ElapsedRefreshIntervals(nowUtc, state)` (how many boundaries the absence owes — [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)), `PerRefreshRate(state)` / `AdvanceAccumulator(...)` (the [#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543) per-refresh trickle amount and its whole-quest error-diffusion step, which advances N intervals in one floored step), `TimeUntilNextQuest(state)` (the honest countdown value — [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)), and `EligibleSubjectPool(tag, state)` (the population-gated purchasable subject pool — see [Cost tiers](#cost-tiers-population-gated-317)) — live in one Unity-independent Core class that `QuestManager` asks, rather than reading raw constants inline. All take `GameState`, so scaling rules driven by dog population change here only, never in the quest engine or the Unity layer.
    - **First launch is the exception**: the trickle rotation is suppressed during [onboarding](../onboarding.md#first-launch-quest-seeding-312) — the world seeds exactly one easy lost-item quest so the tutorial has a single gentle target. Suppression now extends across the whole [onboarding reward-chain](../onboarding.md#onboarding-reward-chain-316): the first normal rotation is held back until the player finishes the four guided steps (quest → upgrade → expand → build), then released the moment the chain completes at the build step. **The release seeds an immediate batch, not a single refresh's trickle ([#579](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/579)).** If it ran one refresh from an empty board, a starting neighborhood (`target` 5 → 0.3125 per refresh, accumulator at 0) would add **nothing at all** — onboarding would end on a near-empty board that only trickles up over the following hours, leaving little to do. Instead, `ReleaseInitialRotation` requests the full `TargetActiveCount` in one shot — skipping the per-refresh accumulator throttle for this one release event only — so the player finishes onboarding to a populated board (up to `target` = 5 ready dogs on distinct houses at the starting population). The same headroom cap, free-dog cap, and ≥1-free-quest guarantee still apply, so the seed can't exceed the target, double-book a dog, or leave an all-paid set; the accumulator and rotation clock are left untouched, so the recurring refresh ([#310](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/310) / [#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543)) then continues the normal trickle cleanly from that seeded set (headroom already met, so the next boundary adds nothing extra). The single Core decision `QuestManager.EnsureQuestsForLaunch` picks the pre-chain seed / mid-chain suppression / post-chain refresh at each launch. ([#312](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/312), [#316](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/316), [#579](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/579))
- **Permanence**: items/decorations delivered or found through completed quests stay in the world permanently — nothing resets with the daily rotation. ([#27](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/27))
- **No pressure**: quests never expire and there is no timer or fail condition anywhere. ([#28](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/28))

**Invariant — the neighborhood is durable.** Anything the player earned, spent, or was given — every dog living in the neighborhood, every offered or accepted quest, every coin — survives closing the app. Only *presentation* is session-only: positions, animations, and in-flight view objects. ([#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704))

**Invariant — a relaunch is never a reset and never a shortcut.** Closing and reopening the app removes no active quest and adds none that waiting wouldn't have. Quest pacing is measured in elapsed time, never in launches. This is what rules out the technically-correct-but-wrong fix of "just seed a full batch at launch," which would restore a populated board while quietly turning relaunching into the fastest way to farm quests. ([#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704))

**Invariant — a restored quest is always something the game can still finish.** No quest is ever loaded into a state whose only continuation was a scene object that did not survive the relaunch. A delivery waiting on a truck is resumed at its walk-home leg — same accepted, already-paid job, rejoined at the last step something still drives — because the truck itself is presentation and is gone. Nothing is re-rolled and nothing is re-charged. ([#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704))

**Invariant — the pacing window is the ceiling; the interval is only granularity** ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)). The board reaches its active-quest target exactly one pacing window after it drops below target, whatever the refresh interval is set to. The interval decides only how granular the trickle is — never the total; where it does not divide the window evenly it may round the fill *moment* to the next boundary, never the total higher. This is why no "max offline hours" constant exists and none may be added: the accrual rate reaches the cap on its own, so a long absence is bounded by arithmetic rather than by a clamp. A fix that caps elapsed time instead of relying on the window is not acceptable, even if it happens to produce a full board.

**Invariant — elapsed real time comes only from persisted UTC timestamps** ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)). The game never assumes it was running while it was closed, and never reads `DateTime.Now` or `TimeZoneInfo.Local` — `nowUtc` is injected and UTC throughout, and the only clock it trusts is the one it saved. Changing the device timezone can neither double-fire nor stall the boundary; moving the device clock *backwards* clamps to zero elapsed intervals rather than subtracting.

**Invariant — every elapsed refresh interval is paid out exactly once** ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)). None is skipped because the app was closed, and none is paid twice. Being away for a pacing window and sitting in the game through one land in the same place, and the partial interval a player returns in the middle of carries to the next boundary rather than being swallowed or re-counted.

**Invariant — time away only ever adds** ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)). Nothing expires, is removed, or fails while the player is gone. Coming back is always a gift, never a bill — [#28](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/28)'s no-pressure rule is untouched by the catch-up.

> **How the spec is changing ([#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743)).** It used to say that missed time is a **single top-up** — away 1 hour or 4 days, returning triggers exactly one refresh — and that the refresh interval is **1 hour** → it now says **every** refresh interval that elapsed while the game was closed pays out, and the interval is **15 minutes** (the pacing window stays 4 hours) → because the old rule made the neighborhood feel paused until you looked at it: closing the game for a day refilled the board by exactly the same one refresh as stepping away for an hour, and the clock then restarted from scratch. [#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704) chose that rule to avoid a catch-up flood and to stop relaunching being a shortcut; both concerns turn out to be handled structurally instead — one pacing window's worth of refreshes already accrues exactly the target, so however long the absence the board simply fills and stops. Shrinking the interval to 15 minutes is the same total delivered more smoothly: identical cumulative counts at every whole hour, but a busy neighborhood sees its first quest at 30 or 45 minutes instead of always waiting an hour. Derek's ruling, 2026-08-25.

> **How the spec is changing ([#704](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/704)).** It used to say active quests need no persistence because "the daily rotation regenerates them, and nothing is lost since quests never expire," and that the hourly clock runs from the last rotation → it now says the board itself is saved, and the clock starts only when the board drops below its target and is re-checked while you play → because the "regenerated by the rotation" reasoning stopped holding the moment a refresh became an hourly *trickle* of one instead of a batch: every relaunch wiped the board and refilled a single quest, which read as "quests take forever to show up." An accepted paid quest made it worse — the coins were deducted durably while the job that reimburses them was not. See [Saved state](../product-scope.md#saved-state) for what is durable and what is deliberately not.

## Currency

*Sources/sinks reframed by [#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626) (v0.13) — see the "getting hired" model below.*

- **Sources**: currency is earned only by completing dog requests/quests — and, since [#675](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/675), by the households that arrive off those completions. No idle income, ads, or mini-games for v1.0 ([#24](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/24)). There are three earn shapes:
    - **Free-type quests** (LostItem / PestControl) — no item to buy, so they pay a **flat** reward (20 coins, see [Numbers](#numbers-placeholder-expect-tuning)).
    - **Paid-type quests** (BuyGift / DecorationRequest / fence) — the **"getting hired to do it" model** ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626)): you **front** the item cost when you accept, then completion **reimburses it with a margin** — `round(cost × markup)`, default markup **1.5×**. Every paid job is therefore **net positive** (toy 30 → +15, pool 50 → +25, fence 100 → +50), and a bigger stake earns a bigger fee. This *inverts* the pre-#626 model, where gifts/decorations were a coin **sink**.
    - **Move-ins** ([#675](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/675)) — a household that fills a vacant house pays a flat **50 coins**, once per household. It is not a separate faucet: a move-in is rolled *on quest completion*, so this income is rate-limited by quest throughput exactly like the two above, and it dilutes on its own as the neighborhood grows (the move-in chance falls with population while quest income rises).
- **Sinks**: the only real coin sinks are **neighborhood expansion** — new streets/houses and house upgrades ([Neighborhood Expansion](../expansion.md)). ([#25](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/25), [#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626))
- **No faucet**: income stays bounded by the board cap + the refresh trickle — each quest pays **once**, at the rate quests appear, never on demand. Accepting a paid quest still costs coins up front, so a broke player must first earn the stake; the [≥1-free-quest guarantee](#core-loop) is the 0-coin bootstrap that always lets them do so. There is **no on-completion replenish and no instant-repeat path** ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626)).

### Numbers (placeholder, expect tuning)

*Source: [#62](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/62)*

> **How the spec is changing ([#742](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/742)).** Both worked payout examples — here and under [Currency](#currency) — used to price the pool at **40 coins** ("pool 40 → 60 / +20") → they now price it at **50** ("pool 50 → 75 / +25") → because 40 was a stale figure the game never charged: `ItemCatalog` has priced the pool at 50 all along, and Derek ruled the code is the correct side. Nothing about the pool changes; only the numbers the spec quotes. 50 sits in the same starter band, so eligibility and the population gate are untouched. A Core test now pins the catalog price and its worked payout to these figures so the two cannot drift apart again.

- A completed **free-type** quest (LostItem / PestControl) pays a **flat 20 coins** (`EconomyNumbers.QuestPayout`). *(#623 raised this from 10 to roughly double the early-game earn rate, which comes only from the free quest types; the value lives on `TuningConfig` so it is slider-tunable in the debug menu.)*
- A completed **paid-type** quest (BuyGift / DecorationRequest / fence) pays **`round(cost × markup)`**, default markup **1.5×** (`EconomyNumbers.PaidQuestPayout`, drawing `TuningConfig.PaidQuestMarkup`) — the [earner model](#currency) above. The item cost is spent on accept and reimbursed-with-margin on completion, so the **net** gain is `payout − cost` (toy 30 → 45 / +15, pool 50 → 75 / +25, fence 100 → 150 / +50). A tested invariant guarantees `payout > cost` for every paid catalog entry, so a paid quest is never a net loss. *(#626; markup is slider-tunable in the debug menu.)*
- A completed **move-in** pays a flat **50 coins** (`EconomyNumbers.MoveInReward`) — **per household, not per dog**, since one move-in fills exactly one house. 50 is exactly the base house-build cost below, so a new neighbour pays for the next house. It is the second coin *source* alongside quests, deposited through the same wallet path on the move-in itself; it cannot be farmed, because a move-in is rolled on quest completion rather than on construction (the [rate-limit invariant](../expansion.md#move-in-system)). *(#675; slider-tunable in the debug menu.)*
- A typical gift/decoration item costs **roughly 30-50 coins** — the **starter cost tier**, the only tier eligible early on (see [Cost tiers](#cost-tiers-population-gated-317) below). Post-#626 this is the size of the **upfront stake** you front, not a permanent loss.

Population-gated cost tiers *(decision 2026-07-28, Derek — see [#317](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/317))*:

- Purchasable-quest difficulty scales with **total dog population** (`state.Dogs.Count`) — as the neighborhood grows, higher-cost tiers become eligible. All boundaries are draft placeholders, named `QuestCostTiers` constants (one-line tunes):

    | Tier | Cost band | Eligible once `Dogs.Count` ≥ |
    |------|-----------|------------------------------|
    | **Starter** | 30-50 coins (today's baseline) | 1 (always) |
    | **Mid** | 60-90 coins | 5 |
    | **Premium** | 100+ coins (no ceiling) | 10 |

v0.4 expansion sinks *(decisions 2026-07-14, Derek; tile/build costs rebalanced 2026-08-03 on [#540](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/540) — see [Neighborhood Expansion](../expansion.md#pricing))*:

- Tile unlock (frontier): **200-coin base + 10 per already-unlocked tile** (`TileUnlock.Cost`), no cap — the first unlock costs roughly what it costs to *fill* the tile it opens (a four-lot tile is 4 × a 50-coin house), then climbs by 10 each. The origin FourWay is excluded, so the first unlock is at the base. *(#540 cheapened the base from a flat 100 and added the scaling; [#674](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/674) then raised the base 50 → 200 so expanding no longer beats building — see [Neighborhood Expansion → pricing](../expansion.md#pricing). This supersedes the retired sequential zone formula 100/200/300…)*
- Building a house: **50-coin base + 5 per 4 houses built** (`HouseBuildNumbers.Cost`), no cap — builds 1-4 at 50, 5-8 at 55, and so on. The 4 starting houses are excluded, so the first build is at the base. *(#540 added the scaling; the base is unchanged.)*
- House upgrades: **100 / 200 / 400 coins** for levels 2 / 3 / 4.

> **#540 in plain English (tile price since superseded by #674):** the very first expansion was made as cheap as a house (50, not 100), so a new player gets moving faster; from there both sinks rise gently so growth can't run away — tiles +10 each, houses +5 every 4. Only player actions count toward the curves (the starting FourWay and the 4 starting houses are excluded), and neither curve is capped.

> **How the spec is changing (#675).** The economy used to have exactly one coin source — completing quests, in two shapes — and a move-in was pure population growth that paid nothing → a completed move-in now also pays a flat **50 coins**, once per household, making arrivals a third earn shape → because 50 is exactly what a house costs to build, so a new neighbour pays for the next house, and the early game needs that push now that a tile unlock costs 200 ([#674](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/674)). It is not a new faucet: move-ins are rolled on quest completion, so the [rate-limit invariant](../expansion.md#move-in-system) keeps the income tied to quest throughput and building houses can never farm it.

> **#674 in plain English:** at 50 a tile, expanding was always cheaper than filling the tile you already had, so the map spread out with empty lots everywhere and the dog population lagged. The unlock now costs **200** — about what four houses cost — so filling a tile before buying the next one is the natural move. The onboarding reward moved to **200** in the same breath, because the guided chain has to pay for its own expand step (below). Nothing else moved: house builds stay 50 (+5 per 4), upgrades stay 100/200/400, and a free quest still pays 20.

Onboarding reward-chain *(decision 2026-07-28, Derek — see [Onboarding](../onboarding.md#onboarding-reward-chain-316))*:

- Each of the four guided steps pays a flat **200 coins** (`OnboardingRewardChainNumbers.RewardPerStep`, raised from 100 by [#674](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/674)), reusing the quest reward-payout path (a wallet deposit), not the random rotation. The ladder is **self-funding**: each step's spend comes out of the rewards banked *before* it, so a player who has earned nothing else clears 100 (upgrade) → 200 (tile unlock) → 50 (build) and finishes on a 450-coin cushion. The binding rung is the expand step — the player reaches it holding `2R − 100`, which must cover the unlock, so a 200 unlock needs a reward of at least **150**. This is an [invariant](../onboarding.md#onboarding-reward-chain-316), not a coincidence: Core derives the minimum viable reward from the **live** sink prices and a test pins the shipped value at or above it. See [Neighborhood Expansion → pricing](../expansion.md#pricing) for the matching sink costs.

These are starting values, not final balance — expect to tune once the daily-rotation pacing can actually be felt in a playable build. Every one of them is a named constant in Core so playtesting adjustments are one-line changes.

## Item catalog

*Source: [#190](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/190), decided in interview with Derek 2026-07-16.*

The priced catalog and every quest type's subject pool are **one source of truth**: a single tagged item catalog (`ItemCatalog`), not per-type hand-maintained lists. Each entry carries:

- **Name**
- **Cost** — optional. Purchasable items (gift/decoration) carry a cost in the 30-50 range above; find-only items (e.g. a lost puppy) carry none, since they're found rather than bought.
- **Eligibility tags** for which quest type(s) it can appear in — Lost, Gift, Decoration. An item can carry more than one tag (a toy or ball is both lost- and gift-eligible).

Each quest type's subject pool is a query over the catalog's tags (e.g. "every Gift-eligible item"), and the decoration-request options are the Decoration-eligible slice of the same catalog — there is no second, independently maintained item list anywhere. Adding a new item is a single catalog entry with its tags and cost; it then appears automatically in every rotation pool it's tagged for. The 30-50 coin rule is a tested invariant on every Gift- or Decoration-eligible catalog entry priced in the **starter tier**; a purchasable entry may instead sit in a higher [cost tier](#cost-tiers-population-gated-317) (mid 60-90, premium 100+), in which case the population gate holds it out of the pool until the neighborhood grows.

The **fence** ([#318](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/318)) is the first such higher-tier entry: a `Gift`-tagged catalog item costing **100 coins** (`ItemCatalog.FenceItemName`/`FenceCost`), reusing the existing Gift eligibility tag — **no new quest type and no fourth eligibility tag**. Its 100-coin price places it in the **premium tier**, so `QuestPacingPolicy.EligibleSubjectPool` only offers it once the neighborhood reaches the premium population gate (~10 dogs) — that population gate *is* its "later game" gate, with no bespoke threshold. Unlike every other Gift subject (a package the delivery truck drops at the door), the fence has **no delivery flow**: accepting it deducts the 100 coins and completes the quest immediately — no delivery truck spawns, the dog does not walk home, and the fence becomes visible right away (no animation). As a paid (Gift-tagged) job it is also an [earner](#currency): the immediate completion pays back `round(100 × 1.5)` = **150 coins** (net +50), the same cost × markup rule as any other paid quest ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626)). Completion records a permanent `PlacedItem(houseId, "fence")` (the same persistence mechanism as any delivered gift, round-tripped by `SaveCodec`); the world's [backyard fence](../world/world.md#backyard-fences) for that lot then renders from that placed item.

**Invariant — a house never owns more than one of the same purchasable Gift item.** A delivered gift is permanent world state (`PlacedItems`), so the Gift subject pool is filtered **per house** before a subject is picked: never an item that house already owns, and never one it already has coming — an offered or accepted `BuyGift` quest that has not been delivered yet, on *any* of that house's dogs. So one yard is never handed a second pool to buy, or a second fence; and a house with nothing left it can be offered is given a different kind of quest rather than a duplicate. The rule is the general "never re-offer something already owned", not a per-item carve-out. ([#742](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/742))

### Cost tiers — population-gated (#317)

*Source: [#317](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/317), decision 2026-07-28 (Derek).*

Purchasable-quest **stake** ramps with the neighborhood's size so a new player is never offered a request whose upfront cost they can't yet front. This is **new cost tiers within the existing 3 quest types**, *not* new quest mechanics — the [frozen-types rule](quest-content.md#v10-quest-types) holds. There is **no hidden level and no new persisted state**: total dog population (`state.Dogs.Count`) is already known, and drives everything.

> **Reframed by [#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626).** Since paid quests are [earners](#currency), a higher tier is a **bigger upfront stake *and* a bigger payback** (`round(cost × 1.5)`), not "unaffordable difficulty". The gate exists so a new player isn't asked to front more coins than they've banked — once they can afford the stake, the pricier job also pays the larger fee. Growth gates the *size of the deal*, not access to profit.

- A **cost tier** is a cost band over the existing item catalog — nothing but a classification of entries already priced. The bands and the population gate that unlocks each are in the [Numbers § table](#numbers-placeholder-expect-tuning) above.
- Eligibility is **cumulative and monotonic** — reaching a higher population only ever *adds* tiers to the eligible pool, never removes the cheaper ones, so an established player still gets affordable requests mixed in. The pure `QuestCostTiers.EligibleCostTiers(dogCount)` function computes this.
- The gate filters only the **purchasable subject pool** — BuyGift subjects and decoration-request options. Catalog entries priced above the population-eligible ceiling are excluded from the candidate set, and the injectable RNG picks only from the eligible slice (deterministic per seed). **Find-only LostItem subjects (no cost) and PestControl (no item cost) are unaffected.**
- At the starting population the eligible pool is **identical to today's** (starter tier only), so onboarding and early game are unchanged.
- The filter is consumed through the [pacing seam](#core-loop)'s `QuestPacingPolicy.EligibleSubjectPool(tag, state)`, which feeds the live `ItemCatalog` and `state.Dogs.Count` into `QuestCostTiers` — one population source of truth shared with [#310](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/310)'s availability pacing, not a second parallel signal.

## Quest authoring

*Source: [#61](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/61), implementation tracked in [#69](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/69). Line-variety model ("Model 2") decided in interview with Derek 2026-07-16, tracked in [#189](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/189).*

Quests are authored as reusable **templates**, not hand-written per dog. Each quest type (see [Quest Content](quest-content.md)) has a dialogue template with variable slots:

- Dog name
- Item/subject (toy name, decoration type, pest type, etc.)
- Personality ([Dog Behavior](../dogs/behavior.md)) — seasons which opener/closer line is drawn, per the pooled model below

New quest types (mechanics) beyond the existing set stay deferred to a later version — this section is only about line variety within the existing types, not new mechanics.

**Templates are selected by type *and* subject** ([#701](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/701)). Almost every subject speaks with its type's template. A subject whose **resolution mechanic differs from its type's default** carries its own pools instead — today only the fence, a "Buy something" purchase that installs in place with no delivery leg ([#318](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/318)). This is not a new quest type or a new mechanic: it is the same type, rendered with lines that describe what it actually does, per the [dialogue-matches-mechanic invariant](quest-content.md#2-buy-something). A new subject-level exception is expected to bring its own pools with it rather than reuse the type's.

> **How the spec is changing (#701).** Authoring used to say each **quest type** has a dialogue template, chosen by type alone → it now says a template is chosen by **type and subject**, with a subject whose mechanic differs from its type's default carrying its own pools → because the fence is a "Buy something" quest with no delivery leg, and inheriting the type's pool had it promising a delivery truck that never comes and describing a lot fixture as a portable gift. Type-alone selection had no seam for a mechanic exception at all, so the wrong promise was already on screen before the engine's fence special-case ran.

### Line variety: pooled openers/closers, uniform random ("Model 2")

For both the opener and the closer, each quest type holds two pools:

- A **default pool** of personality-agnostic lines (authoring target ~10-15 lines).
- A small **per-personality pool** of personality-specific lines, for whichever personalities we choose to flavor (authoring target ~3-4 lines per personality; 0 is valid — flavoring a personality is optional per type).

When a quest fires, the candidate set is `default pool ∪ this dog's personality pool`, and one line is picked **uniformly at random per string** (not per bucket) — so with ~15 defaults and ~3 Grumpy-specific lines, a Grumpy dog says a Grumpy-specific line only ~1-in-6 of the time. Personality is seasoning, not the default voice; wanting more personality presence means writing more personality-specific lines, never special-casing the weighting. Uniform-per-string is a hard requirement — no single line variant may be allowed to dominate.

Selection is **pure random each fire**: no "avoid immediate repeat" memory, no cycle-through, no per-dog or per-session persisted state. The RNG is injectable (constructor/method-seeded `System.Random`), matching the pattern used by the [move-in system](../expansion.md#move-in-system), so line selection stays deterministic under test.

This generalizes what earlier drafts did with a single default line and a single flavored line per personality — same slots, same personality-driven flavoring, just pools instead of one-liners so a given dog doesn't say the identical sentence every time.

**Active-quest reminder pool ([#472](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/472)).** Each quest type carries a third pooled line set — a **reminder** — under the identical Model 2 rules (default pool ∪ this dog's personality pool, uniform-random per string, `{dog}`/`{item}` slots, injectable RNG, no persisted state). It renders as a single line, shown when a dog with an already-`Accepted` quest is re-tapped (see [Conversation System](conversation-system.md) and the [conversation panel](../ui/conversation-panel.md)); the opener/closer offer is unchanged. Each template also declares **who owes the next action** on its accepted quest — the player (lost item, bug problem) or the game (a buy-gift or decoration request, already paid for and in delivery) — and that single declaration drives both the reminder pool's voice and the dismiss pill's label, so the two can never disagree ([#708](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/708)).

## Build checklist

- [ ] Currency balance persists across sessions
- [ ] Completing a **free-type** quest (LostItem / PestControl) grants a flat 20 coins; completing a **paid-type** quest (BuyGift / DecorationRequest / fence) pays `round(cost × markup)` (default 1.5×) so it is net-positive — the [earner model](#currency) ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626))
- [x] Paid quests are earners: BuyGift/DecorationRequest/fence completion pays `round(cost × TuningConfig.PaidQuestMarkup)`, every paid catalog entry pays back more than its cost (invariant), free types keep the flat payout, and no on-completion faucet/instant-replenish path is introduced ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626))
- [ ] Accepting a gift/decoration deducts its cost (30-50 coin range) up front and fails gracefully if the player can't afford it
- [x] A rotation system trickles quests in on a recurring boundary toward a population-scaled cap (`clamp(round(dogCount/3), 5, 12)`), adding `target × RefreshIntervalMinutes / (PacingWindowHours × 60)` per refresh via a persisted fractional error-diffusion accumulator (`GameState.QuestPacingAccumulator`, round-tripped by `SaveCodec`, legacy saves default to 0.0), guaranteeing at least one free-type quest on any refresh that adds a quest so the player is never soft-locked — the cadence, cap, and per-refresh trickle all live behind the `QuestPacingPolicy` seam ([#310](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/310), [#543](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/543), [#743](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/743))
