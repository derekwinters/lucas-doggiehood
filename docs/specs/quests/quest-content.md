# Quest Content

*Epic: [#5](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/5)*

## v1.0 quest types

v1.0 ships with exactly 3 quest types. Expanding variety is explicitly deferred — see [#60](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/60).

!!! note "Cost tiers are not new quest types"
    The population-gated [cost tiers](economy.md#cost-tiers-population-gated-317) ([#317](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/317)) add **higher-cost bands within these same 3 quest types** as the neighborhood grows — bigger-ticket BuyGift and decoration-request subjects, nothing more. They are **not** new quest mechanics, so this frozen-types rule is unaffected: no new resolution mechanic, screen, or interaction is introduced, only a wider price range on the existing purchasable subjects.

### 1. Lost something

*[#12](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/12)*

A dog's conversation reveals it has lost something — a toy, or its own puppy — and needs the player's help finding it. Resolved via the hidden-object search mechanic: the item is placed somewhere visible in the main neighborhood scene (behind a bush, on a roof, etc.); the player pans/zooms around and taps it when spotted. No separate hidden-object scene, hint system, or radar. ([#31](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/31))

!!! note "Eligibility — a puppy can't lose its own puppy ([#463](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/463))"
    The lost-**puppy** subject is excluded when the receiving dog is itself a puppy (`Dog.IsPuppy`), so a puppy is never handed a quest about losing its own puppy. Only the puppy subject is filtered out for puppy receivers — the rest of the Lost pool (toy, ball) remains, so the pool never empties. Non-puppy dogs are unaffected and can still lose a puppy. The excluded name keys off the shared `ItemCatalog.PuppyItemName` catalog constant, not a duplicated literal (#161).

The hidden item's position is generated in Core within the **quest dog's own home tile** — sampled inside that lot's quadrant bounds (`LotBounds.QuadrantBounds`, keyed off `dog.HouseId` → its lot) rather than a fixed origin-centered square — so as the map grows the item is always findable near that dog's house rather than drifting off somewhere in the neighborhood ([#520](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/520)). Within that tile the item is kept clear of both the dog's house footprint **and** the paved street corridor: the sample region is the lot's quadrant bounds with the road corridor cleared (`LotBounds.ClearRoadCorridors` against the lot's own tile roads, `LotBounds.RoadsFor` — tile-aware so a zone lot excludes its own tile's road, not only the origin streets), and candidates within a named house-clearance buffer (`QuestManager.HouseClearanceBuffer`) of that lot's house are then rejected and re-rolled. So the item always sits on open, tappable yard/grass ground — never on the road, grass verge, or sidewalk (which would collide with the on-road delivery truck, [#538](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/538)), and never behind the house where the geometry occludes the tap. The road-corridor exclusion reuses the exact `StreetCorridorInset` (road half-width + verge + sidewalk) yard landscaping already clears ([#244](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/244)/[#455](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/455)), so raw quadrant bounds — whose inner edges sit on the road centerlines — never leak a lost item into the road ([#606](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/606)). (Only that lot's own footprint needs checking — quadrant bounds tile the map with no overlap, so no neighbouring house can intrude on the sampled region.) The buffer clears the full lost-item tap radius with margin, and placement stays deterministic per quest/seed, always resolving to a valid point inside the cleared region. This mirrors the collision-aware footprint check yard landscaping already uses (see [World](../world/world.md) § Yard landscaping). ([#290](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/290))

Tapping is forgiving on two levels, mirroring how the dog speech bubble already handles imprecise touch input: the game-logic tolerance (`QuestManager.LostItemTapRadius`) accepts any tap within 1.5m of the hidden item, and the item also offers a padded screen-space tap zone so that tolerance genuinely applies to real taps — a mouse cursor is pixel-precise, a finger touch is not, and the item's small on-screen footprint under the fixed camera pitch would otherwise leave a raw hit-test with no forgiveness at all. ([#311](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/311))

**How the item renders.** A lost **puppy** reuses the shared Kenney Cube Pets dog model — the same asset every roster dog renders (see [Art & UI Style](../world/art-style.md)) — rather than a bespoke model or the graybox sphere: it is tinted a puppy coat, scaled slightly smaller than a puppy dog (`LostItemView.PuppyModelScale`, below the 0.55 puppy-dog scale), given a fitted tap collider (the imported FBX ships none), and left in place with a slow in-place look-around yaw so it reads as a real puppy at its small on-screen size. Every other subject (e.g. toy, ball — no reusable model yet) still renders the graybox sphere placeholder. ([#335](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/335))

**Finder glow (making the item pop).** The hidden item can visually vanish into its surface — a white ball on the white sidewalk (`SidewalkHex #EFE8D8`), or a tennis-ball-style item in the grass (`GrassHex #7ED957`) — so, regardless of which model renders, the item carries a world-space **finder glow** so it's easy to spot once you're near (Derek, 2026-08-02: *"Option 1 — a world-space glow on the item itself, colored RED"*). The glow is a single flat **red ground ring** on the surface directly beneath the item — nothing else. It is a **ring outline** (a hollow annulus), not a filled disc: a filled disc reads as a painted patch that tints and obscures the ground beneath it, whereas the outline frames the object — clearly saying "this one" while leaving the object and the ground inside the ring uncovered ([#602](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/602)). The ring's **outer** edge keeps the same footprint the earlier disc had (`LostItemGlow.GroundRingScale`); only the middle opens up, sized by the named `LostItemGlow.GroundRingInnerScale` (#161). The ring's height tracks the item's **actual surface** via `WalkNetwork.SurfaceHeightAt` (see [Sidewalks → Ground height](../world/sidewalks.md#ground-height-sidewalks-sit-above-the-road)): on grass it stays at the ground plane, but on the kit's raised curb+sidewalk road band it lifts to that band's surface so the ring floats on top rather than rendering buried under the raised mesh — without which an item on the road showed no indicator at all ([#580](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/580)). The item keeps its **own mesh, size and colour**: an early pass ([#521](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/521)) engulfed the item in a soft pulsing red halo with an orbiting sparkle, but in playtest that read as the item itself — and the lost puppy — ballooning into "a big red ball," so per Derek ([#535](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/535)) the halo, the size pulse and the sparkle are dropped, leaving only the ground contact ring; the item is neither scaled nor recoloured. The ring colour is the named `Palette.LostItemGlowHex` (a bright, saturated red, so it reads on the sidewalk, grass and road alike) and every ring dimension is a named constant on `LostItemGlow` (`Doggiehood.Core.Quests`) (#161). The glow is **pure decoration**: it is active only while a lost-item quest's hidden item is placed (`LostItemGlow.ShouldShow`), it is a child of the item view so it is torn down with the item on collect/dismiss, and it carries no collider and is non-interactable — so it never intercepts a tap and the forgiving tap-to-collect zone ([#311](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/311)) stays sized to the item itself. The glow gates purely on quest state, so the lost **puppy** rides the exact same mechanism as a graybox ball — it too keeps its normal model and size with only the ground ring beneath it. Any onboarding quest that reuses the same lost-item view inherits the glow for free. This is a graybox first pass built from a generated flat ring (annulus) mesh (a Unity `Cylinder` primitive can't be hollow), shared with the onboarding target-house highlight via `GroundRingMesh` so the two stay visually consistent; the final low-poly glow VFX is a later polish. The finder glow's own dimensions are **fixed** (`LostItemGlow.GroundRingScale`, sized to a small item) and are unaffected by how a ring attached to a *house-sized* target is sized — that rule (the ring's hole must contain the target's footprint, with a gap) is the shared `TargetRingGeometry`, see [Onboarding → target-house highlight](../onboarding.md#onboarding-reward-chain-316) ([#669](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/669)). Both read the same annulus hole ratio, so neither ring's proportions can drift as a side effect of changing the other. ([#521](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/521), [#535](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/535), [#602](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/602))

### 2. Buy something

*[#13](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/13)*

A dog asks the player to buy it something — a toy, or a pool for its house. Resolved via the delivery-truck mechanic: accepting the quest deducts currency (which is why re-tapping the dog afterwards acknowledges the delivery rather than asking after the purchase — see [Active-quest reminder copy](#authoring) below and the reminder invariant in [Conversation System](conversation-system.md)), a delivery truck animates in and drops the package at the dog's front door, and the dog receives it automatically — no manual placement by the player. The dropped package is only ever a brief visual beat, not a permanent object at the door (see the transient-package invariant below). The dog itself walks home and sits waiting for the truck (see [Dog Behavior](../dogs/behavior.md)). ([#30](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/30))

Once the package is delivered, the dog is handed back to free-roam wander and picks a **fresh** wander target from where it now stands (its own front door) — it does not resume the target it had cached before the walk home, which would send it beelining off the walk network back to its old position ([#470](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/470)).

**Invariant — the delivery truck never leaves the roadway** ([#538](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/538)). The truck approaches **by road**: it **enters off-map at the open road edge nearest the door** and drives **over the live neighborhood road network** to the point on the roadway nearest the dog's front door, where it stops to drop the package, then drives back off-map — **leaving by a *different* open road edge when one is reachable**, otherwise turning around and retracing the way it came. It **never** drives onto a sidewalk, a yard, or a lot, and it stops **short of the waiting dog** rather than overlapping it — the truck stays on the road while the *package* is placed at the door. This is a hard rule, not just a target outcome: an approach that reaches the door by driving across non-road surfaces is not acceptable even if the package ends up in the right place.

**Invariant — the delivery truck keeps to the right-hand lane** ([#672](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/672)). Being *on* the road is not enough: on every road leg the truck drives its own **right-hand lane** — `RoadWidth / 4` off the centerline, on the right for the direction it is travelling — and never straddles or crosses the centerline, leaving the other half of the street clear for oncoming traffic. It **stops to deliver from its own lane**, including when the door is on the far side of the street: the package is carried to the door, the truck does not cross over. Turning around reverses its heading, so it comes back down the **other** side of the street. See [Sidewalks & Walk Network § Lanes](../world/sidewalks.md#lanes-672) for the road-model rule and its width budget; intersection turn geometry is out of scope for the invariant ([#673](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/673)).

**Entering off-map and routing over the live map** ([#599](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/599)). An **off-map road opening** is an outer tile edge that carries a road but has no placed neighbor across it — the same map-boundary road end the [expansion frontier](../expansion.md) already reasons about (`TileMap.HasRoadConnectionAt` / `TileCatalog.HasRoadOn`), not a new boundary concept. The route is computed **on quest initiation** as a single graph search over the current map (`DeliveryTruckRoute` over `MapWalkNetwork.RoadsFrom` for the live `TileMap`), never pre-cached per house — so it is always correct against the map as it has grown, with no invalidation on tile unlock or house build. The **entry** opening is the one nearest the destination door; ties break deterministically (fixed compass order N→E→S→W, then tile coordinate) so routes are reproducible. The **exit** opening is the nearest opening that is *not* the entry; when the entry is the only reachable opening (a spur or cul-de-sac), the truck turns around in place and retraces. That turnaround is a simple in-place reorient for now, kept as tunable logic (`TruckTurnaround`) so it can later swing around an arc — distinct from the *sidewalk* cul-de-sac bulb arc dogs follow ([#581](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/581)), since the truck is on the road centerline.

**The truck is dispatched only on arrival at the door, and only to that door** ([#677](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/677)). The delivery address is the dog's **own front door** — the point the dog is standing on when it reports home, which the [walk home](../world/sidewalks.md#walking-home-routes-over-the-network) guarantees is that door and nothing else. The truck is dispatched at that moment, so "no truck ever came" is always a symptom of the arrival step, never of the truck.

**Invariant — one quest's failure is contained to that quest** ([#677](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/677)). Whatever goes wrong while resolving a quest — an unplannable walk home, an undrivable truck route — is confined to it. Every other quest still advances that same frame, every truck already on the road keeps driving, and the autonomous [comfort-item approaches](../dogs/behavior.md) keep being offered. A single failure never becomes a broken session, and it is never retried silently forever: it is logged and resolved.

**A delivery that cannot be carried out fails safely** ([#677](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/677)). The player has already been charged when the quest is accepted, so the safe outcome is the one they paid for: the item still lands at the house, the quest completes and pays out as usual, and the dog is handed straight back to wander (`QuestManager.FailDelivery`). What it must **never** do is sit — or leave — the dog in the waiting pose: that pose means "at my door, waiting for my delivery", and its only exit is the delivery itself.

**Right-of-way on the road** ([#546](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/546)). While driving its route the truck also never occupies the same point on a crosswalk as a dog: if a dog is already crossing, the truck **pauses at the crosswalk's near edge** and resumes once the dog clears it; if the truck reaches the crosswalk first, a dog about to cross waits at the curb instead. This is first-come yielding shared by every vehicle, not a truck-only behavior — the full rule lives in [Dog Behavior § Yielding at road crossings](../dogs/behavior.md#yielding-at-road-crossings).

**Invariant — a delivered package is transient** ([#703](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/703)). The dropped package exists only for a **short beat** at the door: long enough for the player to see the drop land while the dog is still in the waiting pose, then it removes itself. No package is ever present at a door outside that window, and none survives the truck that dropped it, the quest that ordered it, or a relaunch. The box is **packaging with no state behind it** — the gift itself is the permanent `PlacedItem` recorded on delivery ([#27](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/27)) — so it is **scene state that is deliberately never saved**, and removing it can never cost the player what they paid for. The beat is owned by the **package**, not by the truck or by quest state: drop and receipt happen on the same frame (the drop *is* what completes the quest), and the truck's drive-away time varies with route length, so neither could time the box's exit. Its duration is a single named Core tunable (`DeliveredPackageLifetime.VisibleSeconds`, #161) and each package runs its own beat, so concurrent deliveries never remove one another's box.

**Invariant — nothing inert blocks a tap** ([#703](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/703)). A world object the player cannot meaningfully interact with never occupies a tap target that something interactive sits behind — and a *stubbed* `IInteractable` consumes taps exactly as a real one does. So a package that has served its beat is **removed outright, collider and all**; hiding its mesh while leaving the collider in place is not an acceptable fix, because the doorway would still swallow every tap aimed at the house behind it.

**Concurrent deliveries and car-following** ([#600](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/600)). Because off-map entry ([#599](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/599)) lengthens each drive, more than one "Buy something" delivery can be **in flight at the same time** — the quest system already paces multiple active quests with no single-active cap, so the driving system owns a **set** of active trucks rather than one at a time. Two trucks sharing a crosswalk yield first-come through the same gate above (the second waits); and along the open road a **1-D car-following** rule keeps a follower **one car length** behind the truck ahead, single-file, waiting **one second** after a stopped leader pulls away before it resumes. The rule composes on top of the crosswalk gate and lives in Core — see [Dog Behavior § Vehicle↔vehicle: car-following on shared roads](../dogs/behavior.md#yielding-at-road-crossings).

> **How the spec is changing (#538).** Earlier the truck drove in a straight line from the world edge to the door — cutting across sidewalks and yards and into the waiting dog — because an earlier fix reached for the dog's package acknowledgement by driving the truck *into* the dog. We keep the acknowledgement but put the truck back on the road: it now routes along the roadway and stops at the nearest road point to the door, and the "never leaves the roadway" invariant above makes that a permanent rule. (What happens when a dog stands *on the road* in the truck's path is a separate concern — see [#546](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/546).)

> **How the spec is changing (#672).** The truck spec used to say only that the truck **stays on the roadway** — so driving down the middle of the street, straddling the centre line the whole way, satisfied it → it now says the truck **keeps to the right-hand lane** of whatever leg it is on, and never crosses the centerline → because "on the road" turned out to be too weak a rule to describe real driving: it left no half of the street for anyone coming the other way, and it made a road a single 1-D queue rather than a two-way street. The road model had no lane concept at all before this — every position it could express *was* the centerline — so the tightened invariant is what stops a future fix from putting the truck back in the middle of the road and still passing.

> **How the spec is changing (#677).** The delivery flow used to be described purely as a happy path — accept, dog walks home and sits, truck delivers — with no rule for what happens when a step *can't* be completed → it now says a delivery that cannot be carried out **fails safely** (the paid-for item still arrives, the dog goes back to wandering) and that a failure is **contained to its own quest** → because in playtest one dog "arrived" at the wrong place, the truck dispatched to that place threw, and — with nothing containing it — every later delivery, every truck already driving, and every comfort-item approach stayed broken for the rest of the session. The happy-path-only wording left "what if it throws?" as an implementation detail, which is exactly how a one-quest bug became a whole-session one.

> **How the spec is changing (#703).** The page used to describe the delivery as *"a delivery truck animates in and drops the package at the dog's front door, and the dog receives it automatically"* and then said nothing at all about the parcel afterwards → it now says the dropped package is **transient**: it shows for a short beat and then removes itself, collider and all → because "drops the package" was read as the end of the story, and nothing ever destroyed the box. In playtest a white cube was left standing in the doorway of every house that had ever received a delivery, stacking one per delivery — and because that box carries a tap collider, it quietly swallowed every later tap on that door. The gift is unaffected: it was always the saved `PlacedItem`, never the box.

**Exception — the fence** ([#318](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/318)): a fence is a lot property rather than a delivered package, so its "Buy something" quest has **no delivery leg**. Accepting deducts the cost (100 coins) and completes immediately — no delivery truck, no walk-home — paying back `round(100 × 1.5)` = **150 coins** (net +50) as a paid [earner](economy.md#currency) ([#626](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/626)), and the [backyard fence](../world/world.md#backyard-fences) becomes visible right away with no animation. It is still the same quest type (a `Gift`-tagged catalog purchase), just installed in place instead of delivered; see [Quest & Economy § Item catalog](economy.md#item-catalog). Its **dialogue follows that mechanic rather than the type default** — see the invariant immediately below — and, completing at accept, it never sits `Accepted` and so never shows an active-quest reminder ([#708](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/708)).

**Invariant — a quest's dialogue never promises a mechanic that quest doesn't run** ([#701](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/701)). When a subject-level exception changes a quest type's resolution mechanic, that subject's dialogue is **selected to match the mechanic it actually runs**, never inherited unchanged from the type's default pool. Concretely for the fence: it asks for a fence around its yard and says it goes up right there — no delivery truck, no "I'll head home and wait", and no portable-handed-over-gift framing (*"my next adventure needs a fence"*) for what is a lot fixture. Dialogue is baked at quest-give time, so the match has to be made when the lines are chosen, not patched up later at accept time.

### 3. Bug problem

*[#53](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/53)*

A dog's house has a bug problem; the player helps by spraying to clear it out. While the quest is active, a bug swarm hovers over the affected house so the player can tell which one needs attention; tapping that house sprays it, which clears the swarm and completes the quest. There's no separate spray tool or aiming — the house itself is the tap target. ([#157](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/157))

The indicator must be **clearly visible, not merely present**: a first-time player must be able to tell which house needs spraying without guessing. It is a world-space marker (no HUD overlay), positioned above the house's **actual** roofline — measured from the house mesh, not a blind fixed height — and made deliberately readable under the fixed 45° orthographic camera: a bright, tall beacon topped by a chunky pest swarm, rising far enough to poke into open sky so a tall roof can't occlude it. It stays purely feedback (no collider — the house underneath remains the tap target) and clears the instant the house is sprayed. The current form is graybox; the final low-poly pest art swaps in via [#334](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/334). ([#331](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/331))

## Not a quest type: decoration requests

Decoration requests (a dog wants something comfy for its yard) are handled by the [Decorations](../decorations.md) system — mechanically similar to "buy something" but with a generic prompt and a small set of player-chosen options rather than one named item.

## Authoring

All 3 types are implemented as templates, not one-off hand-written text — see [Quest & Economy § Quest authoring](economy.md#quest-authoring).

Item/subject names for all 3 types (and for decoration requests) are drawn from a single tagged item catalog, not per-type hand-written lists — see [Quest & Economy § Item catalog](economy.md#item-catalog). Find-only subjects (e.g. a lost puppy) carry no price, since they're found rather than bought.

**Active-quest reminder copy ([#472](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/472), [#708](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/708)).** Alongside the opener and closer, each quest type's template carries a third pooled line set — the **reminder** — shown when a dog with an *already-accepted* quest is re-tapped. It uses the exact same authoring model as the opener/closer — a personality-agnostic default pool plus optional per-personality flavor, `{dog}`/`{item}` slots filled from the same catalog, uniform-random per string via the injectable RNG — just a single reminder line rather than a two-line offer. **Its voice is set by who owes the next action** (the reminder invariant in [Conversation System](conversation-system.md)), and every line in a pool speaks in that one voice:

- **The player owes it** — *lost item* (*"Any sign of my {item} yet?"*) and *bug problem* (a bugs-still-here nudge): the dog asks how the hunt is going, and the dismiss pill reads "Still looking".
- **The game owes it** — *buy something* and *decoration request*: accepting **was** the purchase, so the dog acknowledges the item is bought and the truck is coming (*"My {item} is on its way — thanks for ordering it!"*), and the pill reads "On its way". No line in either pool may ask whether the player has obtained the item. A decoration request reminds about the option chosen at accept time; the fence — the one `Gift` subject with no delivery leg — completes at accept and so never reaches a reminder at all.

The current lines are first-draft placeholders; the real writing pass is [#100](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/100).

> **How the spec is changing (#708).** This paragraph used to give *"Any luck getting me that {item}?"* and *"still thinking about that {item}?"* as the model reminder lines for the two purchase types → those lines are gone, and the page now says a purchase-type reminder acknowledges the delivery instead of asking after it → because for those types the cost is deducted and the truck dispatched at accept (see "Buy something" above), so the only moment the player can ever see those lines is *after* they have already paid.

## Build checklist

- [ ] "Lost something" quest: item placed in-scene, resolved by pan/zoom + tap, no separate search screen
- [ ] "Lost something" quest: the hidden position (incl. the lost puppy) sits on open yard/grass ground — cleared of both the house footprint (`QuestManager.HouseClearanceBuffer`) and the paved street corridor (`LotBounds.ClearRoadCorridors`/`RoadsFor`, `StreetCorridorInset`), tile-aware for zone lots — so it never lands in the road/verge/sidewalk in the on-road delivery truck's path ([#606](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/606))
- [ ] "Lost something" quest: the placed item carries a world-space red **finder glow** — a flat **ground ring** on the surface beneath it (`Palette.LostItemGlowHex`), with the item left at its **normal size and colour** (no halo, size pulse or sparkle) — so it pops on any surface while the puppy/ball still reads as itself; active only while the item is placed, non-interactive, torn down with the item ([#521](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/521), [#535](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/535)). The same red ground-ring visual is reused **for free** by onboarding to mark the "fix up a home" upgrade-target house — a non-interactive world-space cue on a house rather than a lost item, sized so the **whole house footprint, corners included, sits inside the ring's hole with a visible gap of open ground**, concentric with the house's mesh — see [Onboarding → target-house highlight](../onboarding.md#onboarding-reward-chain-316) ([#571](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/571), [#669](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/669))
- [ ] "Buy something" quest: accept → currency deducted → delivery truck spawns → dog walks home and sits → truck delivers to the door → dog "receives" it (decoration/item appears)
