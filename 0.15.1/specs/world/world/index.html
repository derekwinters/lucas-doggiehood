    World & Neighborhood¶

    Epic: #2

    Starting scope¶

    The first playable neighborhood is deliberately small: one intersection of two streets, with one house in each of the four quadrants. (#38)

    • 4 houses total to start, each home to a dog household (see Dog Roster & Names)
    • More streets and houses are designed and slated for v0.4 — see Neighborhood Expansion

    Layout¶

    Streets are lined with houses that the player views and explores from above (#7). There is no player-character traversal — see Camera, Navigation & Controls for how the player actually moves through the space.

    House scale¶

    Decision (2026-07-14, Derek, on #145): every City Kit Suburban house model renders at one fixed uniform scale: ×7 — the kit-wide default unless a specific model gets a documented exception (none today). This replaces the earlier rule that normalized each model's max horizontal footprint to a uniform 8m, which gave every model a different scale factor — houses weren't actually at the same scale, so doors read different sizes house to house.

    The constant lives in Core as HousePlacement.KitScale (WorldBuilder.HouseKitScale is the Unity-side alias), and everything downstream — front-setback placement, door world positions, walkway starts, the catalog gallery — derives from it. At ×7 the four starting models' footprints are: building-type-b 12.80×7.98m, building-type-g 10.15×8.25m, building-type-k 6.45×7.14m, building-type-m 10.00×10.00m. ×8 was rejected at the time because building-type-b would have been 14.6m wide against #129's then-current 15m lot-fence square (a 0.5m-margin guard); at ×7 it kept a 1.1m margin. That square is gone — fences were reshaped to house-anchored backyard runs (#146) and then widened to trace the lot boundary (#342, see Backyard fences) — but the ×7 decision stands on its own.

    Upgrading re-aligns the house to its street (#454). A different footprint depth at each upgrade level would leave a taller mesh sitting at the level-1-calibrated pivot — drifting off the sidewalk — if placement stayed level-blind. So the front-setback pivot (HousePlacement.Position/PositionFor), the shared footprint rect (HousePlacement.HouseFootprint), and the door the front walkway meets are all resolved from the house's current level's model (HouseModelCatalog.ForHouse(houseId, level)), keeping the facade exactly FrontSetback from the sidewalk's outer edge at every level and the walkway attached to the door the current mesh actually shows. WalkNetwork.RefreshFrontWalkway(lot, level) re-projects a single lot's walkway edge to the upgraded door, and HouseUpgradeDirector.RefreshHouse runs it alongside the mesh rebuild so mesh, door, and walkway stay in agreement. The lot itself never moves — its quadrant Property bounds and hand-picked centre are level-invariant, so only the house visual and its walkway re-align. (The level-blind HouseModelCatalog.ForHouse(houseId) overload still resolves the level-1 mesh, and remains in use where no level is in hand — e.g. the house placement pivot/facing the backyard-fence boundary rectangle traces around, whose per-level re-centering is #462's territory. The fence's two connectors already track the current level as of #460, and yard trees reserve the max-across-ladder footprint as of #459.)

    Property bounds¶

    Decision (2026-07-20, Derek, on #222): a property (lot) is one quadrant of a tile. On the starting FourWay intersection each of the 4 quadrants is a property; that same quadrant size is the standard for every layout — tighter layouts (the deferred tile catalog) simply fit fewer properties, never a differently-sized one.

    The geometry lives in Core (LotBounds): QuadrantBounds(lot) returns a rectangle sized to one tile-quadrant (half the 60m WorldDimensions.TileSize per side, so 30m×30m) whose N/S/E/W half derives from the lot's own HouseLot.Quadrant, centred on the tile the lot sits on. The tile is the grid tile the lot's HouseLot.Position falls in (rounded to the nearest TileSize step, the inverse of TileGeometry.CenterOf); the hand-picked distance within that tile (the ±14m house-placement anchor described above) still does not affect the bounds — two lots in the same tile-quadrant share them. For the 4 starting lots this rounds to the origin tile, so their bounds are unchanged; a zone lot on its own tile (e.g. the first zone's cul-de-sac at world Z ≈ 60, #405) gets bounds around that tile's quadrant, so its house position falls inside them — earlier the bounds were always centred on the origin, so a zone lot's house sat outside its bounds and FrontYard/BackYard sliced an inverted (empty) rect and threw. Sizing a full tile-quadrant box around the 14m anchor instead of the tile split would overlap the opposite lot's bounds across the road by a meter; centering each quadrant on the tile's own N/S/E/W split makes the 4 starting lots' bounds exactly tile the 60m tile with no gap or overlap between neighbours.

    LotBounds.FrontYard/BackYard split those bounds relative to HousePlacement.FrontFacing, excluding the house footprint — the front yard is the portion between the house's front facade and the yard/pavement line, the back yard the portion between the house's rear wall and the lot's far edge, both spanning the lot's full width. Because a quadrant's bounds tile the whole 60m tile with no gap, the front yard's raw street-side edge sits on the tile centerline where the faced road runs; FrontYard therefore pulls that edge inward by LotBounds.StreetCorridorInset (the road half-width + grass verge + sidewalk width = the sidewalk's outer edge, the same 5.75m boundary house facades set back from, all derived from WorldDimensions) so the region ends where the actual yard begins and never overlaps the road or sidewalk (#244).

    But on the FourWay every quadrant borders two roads — one on each of its inner edges (the tile-centerline edges at X=0 and Z=0) — and #244 only trimmed the faced road, so both yard regions still spanned onto the perpendicular road's centerline and trees landed in it (#272). LotBounds.ClearRoadCorridors closes that: after the facing split, it pulls every yard edge that lies on a road centerline inward by the same StreetCorridorInset, for both the front and back regions. Which edges border a road is derived generically — an edge is trimmed only when it lies on a road's centerline and overlaps that road's finite extent — so the rule survives map expansion rather than hard-coding the two inner quadrant edges, and an edge #244 already inset (a full StreetCorridorInset off the centerline) is left untouched, so the two trims compose without double-insetting. The road list the trim runs against is tile-aware (#455): LotBounds.RoadsFor(lot, tileType) combines the origin FourWay's fixed streets (NeighborhoodLayout.Roads) with the lot's own tile's road geometry — the TileRoadGeometry.SegmentsFor arms (a TileRoadSegment) converted to Road (Road.HalfLength = half the segment length). A zone tile's own road (e.g. a cul-de-sac's single arm) is otherwise never turned into a Road and so was invisible to the clip, so a cul-de-sac kept-quadrant lot's yards were never trimmed against the road they border and trees landed in the paved strip; the 4 starting FourWay lots stay byte-identical because their own tile's arms lie on the same centerlines as those fixed streets. The single-arg LotBounds.FrontYard/BackYard(lot) still clip against NeighborhoodLayout.Roads alone (used by the starting lots); the tile-aware FrontYard/BackYard(lot, tileType) overloads add the lot's own tile road. This is the region Yard landscaping scatters trees into.

    Backyard fences¶

    Decision (2026-07-28, Derek, on #342 adopting #147's settled geometry, superseding the #146/#129 house-width fence): the backyard fence traces the lot boundary, not the house. The fence line leaves one sidewalk-width strip of grass (2m) between the sidewalk and the fence line on every edge. Road-bordering edges are offset from the sidewalk's outer edge (LotBounds.StreetCorridorInset), and neighbour-yard / map edges are offset from the lot (quadrant) boundary — both by that same sidewalk width (#147 offset fix, 2026-07-30, after Derek playtested the earlier build with the fence line landing inside the road: an inner quadrant edge sits ON the road centerline, not on a yard boundary, so a plain lot-boundary offset put a corner lot's side run inside the road). The old corner-lot house-anchoring still dissolves. The shape is five runs, front open: two side runs + one rear run trace the offset boundary rectangle around the back yard, and two short connectors turn perpendicular-inward from each side run's front end (at the house's depth midpoint) to the house side-wall midpoints. The front yard stays open — no fence line crosses the front, so the front walkway (#128) runs door → sidewalk through the open front and never meets a fence. Fences are defined for every lot but hidden by default — a future fence-purchase quest purchases them (#147/#318).

    The geometry lives in Core (LotFence): GeometryFor traces the road-aware inset of the lot's actual LotBounds.QuadrantBounds (#222) — every quadrant edge that lies on a road centerline is first pulled off the sidewalk's outer edge by LotBounds.StreetCorridorInset (reusing the same LotBounds.ClearRoadCorridors precedent that keeps trees out of the road, with the road-bordering edges derived generically from the road set rather than hard-coded), then every edge is inset by LotFence.BoundaryOffset (= WorldDimensions.SidewalkWidth, 2m). It emits five runs that rotate with the house facing (a house facing +X has its side walls along Z): connector → side run → rear run → side run → connector. That facing (and the front-setback position the connectors reach) is resolved from the live map-spanning walk network for a built house — LotFence.GeometryFor(lot, state) reads GameState.WalkNetwork, not the starting-tile NeighborhoodLayout.WalkNetwork singleton — so a zone house's fence rotates with the house's real street-ward facing instead of the pre-rotation Z-sign fallback (#461, fixing fences that stayed at the old orientation after the #430 house-facing fix reached only the house mesh and its walkway). The road-corridor clear on the state/network-aware path is map-spanning the same way: GeometryFor(lot, state) (and the yard-tree pre-bake's GeometryFor(lot, network, tileType)) feeds ClearRoadCorridors the lot's LotBounds.RoadsFor(lot, tileType) — the origin FourWay arms plus the lot's own tile's roads (TileRoadGeometry), the same per-tile source RoadsFor already supplies to keep expansion-tile yard trees out of the road (#455) — not just NeighborhoodLayout.Roads. Without this, an expansion tile's road is absent from the static origin set, so a lot whose side edge borders that tile's road wasn't recognised as road-bordering and its side run landed inside the paved road (#509; the starting FourWay lots are byte-identical, since their tile's arms coincide with the origin streets). The level-blind gallery/queryable GeometryFor(lot) still uses the origin NeighborhoodLayout.Roads — it carries no tile context and describes a starting-lot fence. Each starting lot is a corner lot — one traced side edge borders the perpendicular inner road, so its side run sits StreetCorridorInset + BoundaryOffset (5.75 + 2 = 7.75m) off that road's centerline (one sidewalk-width strip of grass beyond the sidewalk's outer edge), while the rear run and the other side run sit BoundaryOffset inside their map/neighbour edges. The enclosed back yard is therefore 20.25m wide across a 30m quadrant — still far wider than any house footprint (the widening #342 delivers), just no longer reaching into the road on the inner side (#147). The rear run traces the offset boundary near the quadrant's far edge, retiring #146's shallow 7.5m house-anchored rear line. The two short connectors keep the front open by reaching in only at the house's depth midpoint. The lot-free catalog-gallery form (#126) has no adjacent road network, so it applies the plain uniform offset against a standard quadrant (WorldDimensions.TileSize/2 per side, identical for every lot) synthesised around the house, giving a 26m outline — the real offset outline without a per-model boundary. The corner-lot road offset is validated in the Editor via WorldBuilder.ForceFencesVisible (#152).

    Connectors track the house's current level (#460). A house's rendered mesh grows across its upgrade ladder, but the two connectors reach in to the side-wall midpoints — houseCross ± halfWidth, where halfWidth is the scaled FootprintX/2. Sizing that half-width from the level-1 model (as the level-blind GeometryFor(lot) does) leaves the connectors landing inside a wider upgraded house (a collision) or falling short of its wall (a gap), exactly as Derek saw in v0.8 playtesting. The state-aware LotFence.GeometryFor(lot, state) — which RunsFor(lot, state) now calls — resolves the connector footprint from the house's current level via the level-aware HouseModelCatalog.ForHouse(houseId, level) (#454) at GameState.GetHouseLevel, for a starter house's fixed ladder and a zone house's rolled ladder alike. Only the connector halfWidth is level-aware: the house placement pivot/facing and the lot-based offset boundary rectangle stay on their existing (level-blind) resolution — the boundary is a lot property, not a house one, so it is unaffected. At level 1 the geometry is byte-identical to the level-blind form (the as-built #147/#342 contract), and a per-lot manual override (#223) still wins verbatim. So the termination points move the moment the mesh grows, HouseUpgradeDirector.RefreshHouse calls WorldBuilder.RebuildFences right after the upgrade rebuild so they re-render immediately rather than only at the next full world build.

    Fencing is per lot — HouseLot.HasFence, default off since #146 — so a fresh neighborhood renders no fences. A lot becomes fenced two ways, treated additively by LotFence.IsFenced(lot, state): the static HouseLot.HasFence flag, or a purchased fence recorded in world state — a PlacedItem(houseId, "fence") from a completed fence-purchase quest (#318). The geometry stays queryable for a disabled lot: LotFence.GeometryFor always describes the fence (the #147 purchase flow and the #126 gallery need it), while the flag-respecting, state-aware LotFence.RunsFor(lot, state) — empty while a lot has neither fence source — is what WorldBuilder consumes. Because visibility derives from the persisted PlacedItem, a bought fence survives save/load and reappears on the next world build with no extra state; the buy quest has no delivery animation, so the fence simply shows on the accept-time fence refresh (WorldBuilder.RebuildFences). WorldBuilder.ForceFencesVisible is the Editor-check/test seam that builds every fence anyway. The fence build loop (WorldBuilder.BuildFences at world build, RebuildFences on the #219 debug toggle) iterates every built house — the starting four and every house on an unlocked zone, resolved through GameState.GetHouseLot the same way the build/upgrade paths do — so a zone house's fence is defined and rendered too, not only the starting four (#424, which fixed a starting-only loop that left zone houses unfenced; the fence geometry itself became zone-safe once HouseModelCatalog.ForHouse handled a rolled zone model #414 and LotBounds.QuadrantBounds centred on the zone tile #405).

    Per-lot manual override. The auto-derived geometry above is a good default guess that needs no authoring, but a particular house can want a different fence line. HouseLot therefore accepts an optional per-lot fence override (#223) — HouseLot.FenceOverride, a list of FenceRuns, null by default (HasFenceOverride false). With no override — every shipping lot — LotFence.GeometryFor computes the model-derived runs exactly as above, byte-for-byte unchanged. When an override is present, GeometryFor returns those runs verbatim instead. The override changes the fence's shape only, never its visibility: RunsFor still gates on HasFence (and the state-aware RunsFor(lot, state) on IsFenced), so an override with the fence hidden still contributes nothing. To preserve the continuous-runs invariant, an override is validated at HouseLot construction (LotFence.ValidateFenceOverride, mirroring BackyardRuns' argument guards): it must be a non-empty, continuous chain of runs where each run meets the next with no gap — a malformed (gapped or empty) override is rejected. The check is count-agnostic — it accepts the current five-run backyard shape or any other continuous run of side/rear anchors — so it doesn't fight a future reshape. The override is authored per-lot data, not an in-game panel (no UI screen, no wireframe). It coordinates with the per-lot property alignment of #147: both layer on the same GeometryFor seam, so an override sits on top of #147's alignment rather than conflicting with it.

    Visually, fences render as tiled Kenney City Kit Suburban fence pieces at uniform scale ×5 (1.35m tall — half the ground-tile scale, which would read as a 2.7m wall). Core's FenceTiling compresses a whole number of pieces to cover each run exactly (the same compress-to-fit rule as the walkway pavers), so fence ends land precisely on the side-wall connector ends and the offset boundary corners; WorldBuilder instantiates the pieces verbatim and falls back to one thin graybox rail per run when the kit piece can't be loaded. The catalog gallery shows each model's real backyard fence outline from the same Core API (LotFence.BackyardRuns).

    Yard landscaping¶

    Decision (2026-07-20, Derek, on #222's conversation, reworked into #170): yards get low-poly trees/plants placed procedurally — zero per-lot manual authoring, so the whole neighborhood stays agent-buildable and CI-verifiable. Per property: the front yard generates 4 candidate points and shows 1 most of the time, 2 occasionally; the back yard generates 11 candidates and shows 3-5. Selection is seeded/deterministic per lot — the same lot always lands on the same trees across sessions and builds.

    The geometry lives in Core (YardLandscaping), split the same two-tier way as LotFence: pure GenerateFrontCandidates/GenerateBackCandidates/SelectFront/SelectBack overloads take explicit yard regions and obstacles, while the *For(HouseLot) overloads resolve that geometry from NeighborhoodLayout/LotFence and derive the seed from the lot's own HouseLot.HouseId. Candidates are rejection-sampled inside Property bounds' LotBounds.FrontYard/BackYard, collision-aware against the house footprint, the front walkway (#128), the backyard fence line (checked via LotFence.GeometryFor, regardless of whether the fence is currently purchased/visible), and each other. The house obstacle is the max-across-upgrade-ladder footprint (HousePlacement.MaxHouseFootprint), not the as-built level-1 footprint: a tree is seeded once at world-build time while the house is level 1, but the rendered mesh grows across the upgrade ladder from the same fixed centre, so reserving only the level-1 footprint let a tree that was legal when the house was small end up inside the larger upgraded house (#459, v0.8 playtesting). MaxHouseFootprint takes the componentwise max FootprintX/FootprintZ over every rung of the house's ladder (HouseModelCatalog.MaxFootprint, resolving the fixed starter ladder or a zone house's rolled ladder exactly as HouseModelCatalog.ForHouse does) and builds the rect the same way HouseFootprint does (same centre Position(lot, KitScale), same width/depth axis-swap-on-facing). This is scoped to yard trees only — quest hidden-item placement (#290) and the empty-lot foundation slab (#434) keep rejecting against the level-1 HouseFootprint. Candidates additionally stay clear of every road bordering the lot — faced and perpendicular — because the yard regions themselves now end at the paved street corridor (LotBounds.StreetCorridorInset) on every road-bordering edge rather than reaching the tile centerline. Playtesting (#244) first showed front trees escaping into the faced road; a follow-up (#272) showed they still landed in the perpendicular side road, since a quadrant borders two roads and #244 trimmed only the faced one — LotBounds.ClearRoadCorridors (see Property bounds) now trims both, for the front and back regions alike, so a candidate's full scaled footprint clears every road it borders. A third playtest (#455) showed trees still landing in the road on the first unlocked cul-de-sac tile: the clip only knew NeighborhoodLayout.Roads, so a lot on a non-origin tile was never trimmed against its own tile's road. The tile-aware *For(HouseLot, TileType) resolvers close that — they clip against LotBounds.FrontYard/BackYard(lot, tileType), whose road list adds the lot's own tile road (see Property bounds); the Unity layer passes the lot's tile type (from the zone that owns it) when rendering a zone lot's yard, while the starting FourWay lots keep the single-arg resolvers unchanged. A zone lot's trees are also oriented to the house's real street-ward facing (#461): because they are pre-baked at unlock — before the house (and its front-walkway edge) exists (#434/#450) — the *For(HouseLot, TileType, WalkNetwork) resolvers derive the predetermined facing by projecting the lot centre onto its nearest sidewalk in the live network (HousePlacement.PredeterminedFrontFacing/PredeterminedPosition, threaded through LotBounds.FrontYard/BackYard and HousePlacement.MaxHouseFootprint/HouseFootprint). On this axis-aligned map that predetermined facing equals the walkway-derived facing the eventual build resolves, so the pre-baked trees agree with the built house and are never regenerated (a Core test locks the equality, protecting #434's no-regenerate assumption); before the fix they used the Z-sign fallback and sat at the pre-rotation orientation. That agreement covers the facing but, as of #702's sweep, not yet the front-yard's walkway obstacle: the walkway only exists in the network once the house is built, so a front tree on some lots is sampled against a different obstacle set before and after the build and shifts position on the next launch — a known gap tracked in #719, where the choice of which walkway a yard reserves is still open. It does not affect the invariant below (the trees are all still there, and a reload re-renders a built lot's yard unchanged). Every candidate and pick is spaced by YardLandscaping.MinSpacing, two footprint radii derived from the larger canopy half-extent of the two kit tree meshes (tree-large.fbx/tree-small.fbx, which share a canopy footprint) at the module's uniform scale × the worst-case per-tree size (YardLandscaping.MaxTreeScaleVariance, ×1.25 — see the per-tree size variability below), so no placed prop can ever overlap another regardless of which model lands where or how large it renders (#458).

    Decision (2026-07-23, Derek, on #243): the earlier planter kind is removed entirely — playtesting showed planters placed in odd, random yard spots, so the whole code path is gone and a planter can never be selected or rendered. Only the two tree meshes remain; the collision spacing (previously keyed off the planter, the widest piece) is re-derived from the larger tree canopy half-extent.

    Visually, each pick instantiates its selected tree-large/tree-small Kenney City Kit Suburban model at uniform scale ×5 (matching FenceTiling.Scale, since the trees stand alongside the same backyard fences — Derek tunes both further in the Editor check), varied per tree by a size multiplier (YardTreePlacement.Scale) so no two yards read identically. WorldBuilder builds one "Yard - N" container per lot holding its front-then-back picks, falling back to one simple primitive marker per pick (same pattern as the walkways/fences) when a kit piece can't be loaded.

    Invariant — every lot with a house has yard trees in every session, not only the one it was built in (#702). A lot's trees belong to the lot, so which lots get rendered is decided by the lot's own existence on an unlocked tile — never by whether the lot is still free to build on. This is a hard rule, not a target outcome: a render path keyed on "this lot has no house yet" is not acceptable even when it puts trees on screen for the session the player is looking at, because the pre-placed trees are scene state and the next launch rebuilds the world from GameState alone. Concretely, the two render loops together cover every lot exactly once — WorldBuilder.BuildYardLandscaping(Transform) over the four starting NeighborhoodLayout.HouseLots, and WorldBuilder.BuildEmptyLots/RenderUnlockedTile over every lot of every unlocked tile, built or not (the origin FourWay's lots are deliberately excluded from GameState.LotsForUnlockedTile, #607, so the two loops partition rather than overlap). Only the foundation slab stays gated on buildable state — a built lot has a real house mesh and wants no marker under it. The rule extends to the count as well as the render: a lot never selects zero trees. SelectFront/SelectBack cap their pick count at Math.Min(desired, candidates.Count), so a yard whose candidate generation came back empty would render bare and say nothing — every lotted TileType is therefore swept in Core (YardTreeCoverageTests) at both stages, unbuilt and built, and WorldBuilder.RenderYardPicks logs a warning rather than returning silently if an empty pick list ever reaches it.

    How the spec is changing (#702). Yard landscaping used to be specified purely as an outcome — yards get trees, seeded deterministically per lot, "the same lot always lands on the same trees across sessions and builds" — with nothing said about which lots are asked for trees, or when → it now carries the invariant above: a lot's trees follow the lot, are rendered for built and unbuilt lots alike, and are never zero → because the outcome-only wording left "render the trees for lots that don't have a house yet" as a technically-conforming reading, and that is exactly what shipped. Every house the player built outside the starting intersection came back bare after a relaunch, since its trees had only ever been pre-placed into the live scene at unlock time and no path rebuilt them for a lot that now carried a house. Lucas saw the trees when the tile unlocked and they were gone the next time he opened the game.

    Decision (2026-08-01, Derek, on #458, from v0.8 playtesting): yard trees vary in size — up to 25% larger than the base ×5, and never smaller. Each of the two SelectFront/SelectBack picks draws a YardTreePlacement.Scale uniformly from [YardTreePlacement.BaselineScale (1.0), YardLandscaping.MaxTreeScaleVariance (1.25)]using the **same already-seeded per-lotRandom** its position and kind are drawn from, so a lot's sizes are as deterministic as its trees (the [#170](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/170) same-lot→same-yard guarantee, extended to size).WorldBuilder.BuildYardTreerenders the model atUniformScale × Scale(so the base ×5 varies up to **×6.25** per tree), and the collision spacing widens to that worst case (above) so bigger trees still can't overlap. The variability is scoped to the two yard-tree paths only: the **open-space** trees on a tile's dropped (no-lot) quadrants (WorldBuilder.BuildTileOpenSpaceTrees— cul-de-sac bulb sides, bend corners, and twin-bend quadrants alike, [#614](https://github.com/derekwinters/lucas-doggiehood/issues/614)) construct a placement with no lot/seed context, so theirScale` stays at the 1.0 baseline and they render at the fixed base size.

    Open-space environment¶

    Decision (2026-07-31, Derek & Lucas, on #384): the parts of the world that aren't road or lot tiles must read as filled environment, not empty void. For now that fill is grass only — a single flat grass plane covering the map's occupied footprint. Water is out of scope for this issue (deferred to its own future issue), and parks are out of scope too (deferred — "eventually," per the issue; a park's contents are undesigned).

    The fill is not new code — it is exactly the ground the #373 expansion-rendering fix already ships (see Neighborhood Expansion → Expansion indicator, the #373 implementation note). The base grass plane is sized from the live GameState.Map via Doggiehood.Core.Cameras.CameraController.GroundExtentForMap(map) — the map's tile coverage (MapExtent.Covering, a half-tile beyond the outermost tile centres) then padded on every side by a modest constant margin (CameraController.BoundsMargin, 12m — the same margin the pan bounds use) — and it is re-centred and grown whenever a zone is unlocked (WorldBuilder.ResizeGroundToMap). So the plane tracks the map's own footprint plus that fixed margin at every size, staying proportionate to the neighborhood rather than dwarfing it.

    Decision (2026-08-04, Derek, on #558, from v0.11 playtesting): the earlier approach (#536) padded the ground all the way out to the camera's max-zoom-out reach so the near edge never showed the flat blue clear colour at full zoom-out. But that reach grows with the map (camera controls → the maximum zoom-out grows with the map too, #510/#524), so the grass ballooned to ~10× the tile-footprint area on the starting map and grew ~2m for every 1m the map grew — a huge empty field around a small cluster. #558 decouples the ground size from MaxZoom (option (b)): the mesh now pads only by the constant BoundsMargin, and the "never expose the void" guarantee moves to a grass-coloured camera clear colour — the main game camera clears to Palette.GrassHex (#7ED957), the same green the plane is painted, wired in CameraRig.ApplyConfiguration (mirroring PortraitCamera). Any area beyond the mesh edge at an extreme pan+zoom-out then reads as continuous grass rather than a blue seam — a pixel-level backstop that can't under-cover, so shrinking the mesh doesn't reopen the seam #536 fixed. The Main scene's camera is authored with that same SolidColor grass clear so the serialized value matches the ApplyConfiguration runtime intent (no blue skybox-fallback before Awake, and no drift if the runtime override is ever bypassed); it is pinned by a serialization-level guard test (#559).

    So every placed tile, and the open cells around and between the road tiles, sit on Palette.GrassHex grass instead of blank space, all the way to (and past) the authored map's frontier — and the camera's own background is that same grass, so nothing off the frontier ever reads as void. There is no per-cell surface authoring and no water surface: the whole open-space area is the one grass plane.

    Diagnostic toggle (2026-08-06, on #611): because the ground plane (lit) and the void backstop (unlit camera clear) both paint Palette.GrassHex, a zoom-out "border" seam has been hard to attribute to one or the other. The Settings ▸ Debug tab carries a Show debug element colors switch that paints the ground plane and the camera backstop two loudly-different, obviously-fake debug colours (magenta ground vs. cyan backstop, from the Core DebugElementColors seam) so which element is on screen is unambiguous. It is a diagnostic-only dev toggle, not a shipped visual — off by default, reset each session like the rest of the Debug tab, driving WorldBuilder.ShowDebugElementColors; when off, the ground and backstop are byte-identical to the matched grass above. See Settings → Debug pane.

    Scope guardrails for this issue (so a reviewer doesn't look for more):

    • No water surface. There is no water cell type, no per-cell surface field in docs/tools/map-data.json, and no water rendering. Whether water is authored per cell or placed by a positional rule (e.g. a map border ring) is a decision left to a future issue.
    • No parks. No open cell is designated a park, and a park's contents (paths, pond, play equipment) are undesigned. Parks phase in under a separate future issue.
    • Extent = the authored map's frontier plus a constant margin; the void is backstopped by colour, not by over-growing the mesh. The grass covers the occupied TileMap footprint (the starting intersection plus any unlocked zones — and, once the authored map loads in-game via #383, the full neighborhood) padded by the fixed CameraController.BoundsMargin (#558) — no longer padded out to the camera's max-zoom-out reach (#536, superseded). The plane grows and re-centres with the live map (never a fixed oversized pad, never an infinite plane), but the pad itself stays constant so it never dwarfs the neighborhood. Anything beyond the mesh edge at an extreme pan+zoom-out is covered by the grass-coloured camera clear colour rather than a larger mesh.

    Lighting & time¶

    Static, pleasant daytime lighting — always sunny/mid-day. No day/night cycle and no weather system for v1.0. (#39)

    This is a deliberate simplification: it avoids needing lighting variants for every asset, and a future weather/day-night system is tracked separately as an explicit future idea — see Future Ideas.

    Art direction¶

    Low-poly 3D art style throughout — houses, dogs, streets, and props all share this style (#6). The specific color palette and house architecture are specified in Art & UI Style.

    A cover art concept for the game exists at #90 as a visual reference for overall tone.
