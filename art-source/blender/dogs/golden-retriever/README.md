# Golden Retriever (approved, #855)

The roster's Golden Retriever (Bailey; also Sunny as a puppy, and the Arnie / Hank / Brody
easter-egg coats), the first per-breed dog model. Derek approved the round-4 concept on
2026-11-24, and it is kept here as **source assets only** (#855). Nothing here is imported by
Unity, because the folder is outside `Assets/`; the game still uses the Cube Pets dog. Importing it
depends on the glTF importer (#776), and animations come later.

It follows `docs/engineering/blender-pipeline.md`: generated by headless `bpy==5.3.0` scripts,
1 Blender unit = 1 m, front faces -Y, up +Z, glTF 2.0 exported Y-up with `export_apply`, textures
generated with numpy + Pillow (no external images), materials named `DH_Dog_*`, `.gltf` + `.bin`
pointing at `../textures/`, and an export step that reads every file back and exits non-zero if a
check fails.

## The approved model

- **One continuous skinned body.** The torso, neck, head (with the jaw), legs and tail are one
  mesh, `dog_golden_body`, bent by a 32-bone armature with at most 4 weights per vertex. Each body
  shape (lofted torso, head, tail, leg segments) becomes a signed distance field; the fields are
  merged with a **smooth minimum** (a rounded fillet of 3.5–7 cm wherever two shapes meet), meshed
  (surface nets), cleaned (voxel remesh), retopologised to quads with QuadriFlow, re-cut for the
  mouth, unwrapped and re-textured. The ears, eyes, nose, tongue and collar are separate small
  pieces.

  > **Invariant:** nothing a viewer can see is two meshes intersecting along a straight line. Body
  > parts meet through the field's smooth minimum; only the small rigid pieces (eyes, nose, collar,
  > tongue) and the ears are separate.
- **Rig.** Bones: root, hips, spine_1–3, neck, head, jaw, ear_L/R_1–2, tail_1–4, and per leg
  back_hip / back_stifle / back_hock / back_foot and front_shoulder / front_elbow / front_wrist /
  front_paw. The body and the ears are skinned; eyes and nose are parented to `head`, the collar
  to `neck`, the tongue to `jaw`. **No animation clips.** The file's default ("rest") pose opens the
  jaw 12°; the bind pose is standing with the mouth closed.
- **Hind leg.** The thigh angles forward and down from the hip into a rounded haunch, the stifle
  bends, the lower thigh angles back to the hock, and the rear foot rises nearly vertically from the
  paw. The front legs have an elbow at the bottom of the chest and a wrist above a short pastern.
- **Chest.** 0.53 m wide at the shoulder and 0.50 m at the ribs.
- **Mouth.** The lips are cut into the skin from one mouth corner to the other; the corners sit on
  the hinge axis and are shared, so the lips always meet there. The jaw is part of the skin,
  weighted to the `jaw` bone, and the cheeks and throat stretch with it. It opens up to 34° without
  a gap.
- **Sit pose, solved against the skinned mesh** (`dh_dog_rig.SIT`, re-derived by `solve_sit.py`):
  the root drops 0.54 m and the hips tilt 61°, the hind leg lies along the ground (stifle at 10 cm,
  hock 3.9 cm up, rear foot flat), the front legs stay near vertical (8° forward), the tail lies on
  the ground behind, and the neck and head counter-rotate so the head is level.

  > **Invariant:** in the sit pose the rump/haunches, hocks, hind feet, front paws and tail each
  > touch the ground (lowest skinned vertex within 1 cm of z = 0), and no vertex of any piece goes
  > more than 1 cm below it.

  `export_dog.py` checks this on the full model and the LOD and fails the export otherwise. Measured
  (m, + = above the ground; stored in `dog_stats.json` as `sit_contact_m`):

  | Part | Full | LOD1 |
  |---|---:|---:|
  | rump / haunches | +0.0016 | +0.0031 |
  | hocks | +0.0007 | +0.0098 |
  | hind feet | 0.0000 | +0.0034 |
  | front paws | −0.0009 | −0.0010 |
  | tail | 0.0000 | +0.0039 |
  | lowest vertex, any piece | −0.0009 | −0.0010 |

## Pieces and triangles

| Piece | Tris | LOD1 tris | How it moves | Materials |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| body (torso, neck, head + jaw, legs, tail, mouth) | 3,076 | 988 | skinned, 30 bones | Fur, Soft |
| ear_L / ear_R | 96 each | 60 each | skinned, ear_1 + ear_2 | Fur |
| eye_L / eye_R (+ catch-light) | 136 each | 64 each | child of `head` | Gloss |
| nose | 80 | 36 | child of `head` | Gloss |
| collar (+ tag) | 176 | 110 | child of `neck` | Soft, Gloss |
| tongue | 96 | 48 | child of `jaw` | Soft |

**Total: 3,892 triangles** (target 4,000). **LOD1: 1,430** (target 1,500). The art budget itself is
still open in #799. The LOD body is the full retopology decimated (quadric collapse,
mirror-symmetric), because QuadriFlow at LOD counts dropped the tail tip.

**Skin weights.** Each body vertex gets a soft membership in the shapes it was blended from
(exp(−(d − d_min) / 2 cm)). Each shape maps to a bone, or to a chain by position along it (torso →
hips, spine_1–3, neck; tail → tail_1–4). The jaw takes the head's share below the lip plane. The
weights are smoothed over the mesh (length-weighted), and the 4 largest are kept and normalised.

## Coats, materials and textures

**Coats** (main / feathering / ears):

| Coat | Main | Feathering | Ears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | `#D29A50` | `#EACB8E` | `#AE6F32` |
| Default | `#C4843C` | `#E2B472` | `#9A5B26` |
| Dark | `#9C5424` | `#C2814A` | `#723A18` |

The coats are three `.gltf` files with identical `.bin`s (same mesh, rig and UVs; only the
`DH_Dog_Fur_<Coat>` material differs). For Unity that is one model plus three fur materials.

| Material | What uses it | Maps |
|---|---|---|
| `DH_Dog_Fur_Default` / `_Light` / `_Dark` | the fur (one per coat file) | fur colour map + shared fur normal map |
| `DH_Dog_Gloss` | eyes, catch-lights, nose, collar tag (roughness 0.3) | trim palette |
| `DH_Dog_Soft` | lips (black band), inside of the mouth, cheek webs, tongue, collar strap (roughness 0.55) | trim palette |

Nothing is double-sided and nothing is alpha.

| Texture | Size | What |
|---|---|---|
| `dog_fur_<coat>_color.png` | 1024 | the coat colour maps |
| `dog_fur_normal.png` | 1024 | strand normal map, shared by the coats (OpenGL / glTF convention) |
| `dog_fur_mask.png` | 1024 | R = strand detail, G = feathering, B = ear; the coat maps are baked from it |
| `dog_fur_lod1_*` | 512 | the same set for the LOD's own unwrap |
| `dog_trim_color.png` | 256 | palette: eye, catch-light, nose, tag, mouth, tongue, black lip, collar |

**How the fur is baked.** Every texel of a fur triangle gets its 3D position, normal, UV tangent
frame and body-region weights. A periodic, anisotropic strand-noise tile, made at real size, is
sampled in 3D on the two planes that contain the fur's flow direction (back and down on the body and
head, down the legs, along the tail, down the ears). So strands always run along the flow, are never
stretched, and both sides of a UV seam get the same fur. The normal map comes from the same noise's
3D gradient. Feathering (chest, belly, throat, backs of the legs, breeches, tail underside, muzzle,
paws) and the darker ears are 3D rules too. The skin uses about 46% of the atlas.

## Regenerating

Run from the repository root with headless Blender installed as a Python module
(`pip install bpy==5.3.0`; the scripts also use numpy and Pillow):

```
python3 art-source/blender/dogs/golden-retriever/export_dog.py      # textures + exports + checks + stats, about 2 min
python3 art-source/blender/dogs/golden-retriever/render_dog.py <shot> [out_dir]
python3 art-source/blender/dogs/golden-retriever/solve_sit.py       # only after changing the shape or rig
```

- **`export_dog.py`** rewrites `textures/` and `exports/` and prints `ALL OK` (exit 0) when every
  check passes. Two runs give byte-identical textures and exports: QuadriFlow runs with a fixed seed
  on a fixed grid. It also writes `dog-golden-viewer.glb` (one self-contained file for a web
  viewer, checked to be under 3 MB), which is git-ignored.
- **`render_dog.py`** shots: `turnaround gamecam face coats rig pivots splash lod mouth hips joins
  sit chest | all`. Output defaults to `out/` (git-ignored); copy the sheets you want into
  `review/`. `all` takes about 35 min on 4 CPU cores (EEVEE on Mesa's lavapipe, which the script
  selects itself when present). Renders are not byte-reproducible. `gamecam` reads base 1 and the
  street props from `art-source/blender/`. `splash` needs `DH_SPLASH` set to the splash-art golden
  (`dogs/standalone/dog-golden.png`), which is not in the repository. The "before" rows of `mouth`,
  `joins` and `chest` need `DH_DOG_ROUNDS` set to the design folder holding the earlier rounds'
  `round2/scripts` and `round3/scripts`; without it those rows are left empty.
- **`solve_sit.py`** prints a new `SIT` dict to paste into `dh_dog_rig.py`. It is deterministic.

**Checks `export_dog.py` runs on every file** (and the viewer `.glb`):
- images: URIs relative and resolving into `textures/`, or embedded in the `.glb`;
- materials: fur has colour + normal maps, trim has a colour map; nothing double-sided; at most 5
  materials, all `DH_Dog_*`;
- rig: exactly one skin with the 32 expected joints, each with the right parent; inverse bind
  matrix count;
- skinning: body and ears are skinned with `JOINTS_0`/`WEIGHTS_0` only (at most 4 influences) and
  every vertex's weights sum to 1;
- weight sanity: leg bones only on their own side, head and jaw only up at the head, tail only
  behind the hips;
- eyes, nose, collar and tongue are parented to their bones; no animations;
- the default pose, skinned from the file itself, stands on the ground and matches Blender's own
  deformed mesh (bounds per piece within 2 mm);
- triangles within the targets, and Blender's count matches the glTF's;
- the sit-pose invariant above, on the full model and the LOD;
- viewer `.glb` under 3 MB.

**PNG metadata.** No PNG committed here may carry a C2PA (`caBX`) chunk. Some shared folders add one
to every image written to them; regenerate the textures here rather than copying them in, and strip
the chunk from any review sheet copied in (the pixels are unchanged).

## Known limits

- **Topology is retopology, not hand-made.** QuadriFlow gives even quads everywhere, with no
  deliberate edge loops at the joints and no extra density on the face. The muzzle and paws are a
  little lumpy in close-ups; they read fine at the game camera.
- **Skinning is automatic (region-based), not hand-painted.** Extreme poses would show linear-blend
  artefacts, mainly the haunch and flank in a deep sit, and the elbow.
- **The sit is a static test pose, solved for contact.** It is a fairly upright sit; the haunch
  loses a little volume where it meets the ground. The LOD's hocks are the closest to the 1 cm limit
  (9.8 mm), because the LOD is posed through the full model's solution.
- **The ears, collar, eyes and nose are separate pieces** that sit on the skin rather than blend
  into it.
- **Atlas use is about 46%.** A hand unwrap would give about 2× the texel density at the same size.
- **The LOD** is decimated triangles (no quads), and its collar has 10 segments.
