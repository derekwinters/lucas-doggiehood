# Dog toys: five designs (#332)

A lost or delivered "toy" is a plain white graybox sphere today (`LostItemView`). This folder builds
**five real dog toys** in Blender. Derek approved all five as the toy set on #332, and the plush
duck from the first round was dropped. Every toy is generated by a Blender Python script run
headless, with no hand modelling, and follows
[`docs/engineering/blender-pipeline.md`](../../../../docs/engineering/blender-pipeline.md). They are
**source assets only**: nothing here is imported by Unity (the folder is outside `Assets/`).

**How the game picks among the five is not decided here.** Showing a lost toy is #928 and showing a
delivered toy is #929, and those issues decide which design a given toy uses.

## The five toys

| # | Design | Object / files | Size W × L × H (m) | Triangles | Look |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Flying disc | `toy_disc` | 0.45 × 0.45 × 0.05 | 1,440 | Solid bright blue, domed top, a raised ridge and a rolled rim. Solid all the way across, so it can't be mistaken for the red finder ring (#602) |
| 2 | Rubber chew cone | `toy_chew_cone` | 0.23 × 0.23 × 0.28 | 2,752 | Bright purple, three stacked lobes, a hole in the top, standing upright |
| 3 | Plush hot dog | `toy_hotdog` | 0.24 × 0.50 × 0.17 | 1,660 | Two tan bun halves, a red-brown sausage poking out of both ends, a yellow mustard zigzag |
| 4 | Rubber chicken | `toy_rubber_chicken` | 0.21 × 0.81 × 0.14 | 1,532 | Long yellow gag chicken lying flat: neck stretched forward, orange beak and legs, red comb |
| 5 | Knotted rope | `toy_rope_knot` | 0.27 × 0.78 × 0.16 | 4,456 | Two twisted strands (orange and blue), one chunky knot in the middle, frayed tassels at both ends |

Every toy is one mesh, well under the provisional 6,000-triangle per-mesh limit of the art budget
(#799). The colours avoid the lawn's green, the sidewalk's light grey and the red of the finder
ring.

**Size.** Dogs in this world are about 1.8 times real size: the approved adult Labrador is 1.58 m to
the top of its head and 1.04 m at the back, where a real Lab is about 0.57 m at the shoulder. Each
toy is a real toy of its kind at that same scale.

**At the game camera** (see the line-ups):

- At the closest zoom, every toy reads as what it is.
- At the default zoom they are small:
  - the disc is a clear blue oval;
  - the hot dog, chicken and rope are small coloured shapes;
  - the chew cone, the smallest, is a purple dot.

The red finder ring marks a lost toy at any zoom.

**Choices made while building them:**

- **The disc is blue, not yellow.** Yellow would sit close to the tennis-ball colour planned for
  the ball (#333) and stands out less on the light sidewalk.
- **The food toy is a hot dog, not a pizza slice.** The bun, sausage and mustard read as food from
  above and from the side. A flat slice is a thin wedge from the 45° camera.

## Shared conventions (for #928 and #929)

These are the same as the street props:

- 1 Blender unit = 1 m at in-game size, so the game places each toy at **scale 1**.
- Front faces −Y and up is +Z in Blender. The exporter turns that into glTF's Y-up
  (`export_yup`).
- **Pivot on the ground at the centre of the footprint.** Each toy rests on the surface at its
  placement point (lowest point at z = 0). The graybox sphere's `SphereGroundHeight` offset doesn't
  apply.
- Transforms are applied on export. Each file holds one node and one mesh, both named
  `toy_<design>`.
- Materials are named `DH_Toy_<slot>` (for example `DH_Toy_disc_plastic`) and are single-sided.
- The hot dog and the rope are plush or fibre, and they share one tileable fleece texture pair
  (`textures/toy_fleece_{color,normal}.png`, 256²), tinted by each material's `baseColorFactor`.
- The disc, cone and chicken are plain-colour plastic or rubber with no textures.

## Files

```
items/toy/
  dh_toy_tex.py      the fleece texture pair (numpy + PIL, fixed seed 332, tileable)
  dh_toy.py          the shared primitives (ellipsoid, lathe, tube) and the five designs in DESIGNS
  export_toy.py      writes the textures, exports/toy_<design>.gltf + .bin and exports/toy_stats.json,
                     reads every glTF back and exits non-zero if a check fails
  render_toy.py      the review sheets and the web-viewer .glb files, to out/ (git-ignored)
  tests/test_toy.py  budget, pivot, solid disc, read-back and byte-for-byte rebuild checks
  textures/          toy_fleece_color.png, toy_fleece_normal.png
  exports/           toy_<design>.gltf + .bin (images by relative URI ../textures/), toy_stats.json
  review/            the review sheets below
```

The shapes are built vertex by vertex in a fixed order, not with `bmesh.ops.create_uvsphere`. In
`bpy` 5.3.0 that operator gives a different face order from run to run, so the exports changed
every build.

The export checks every toy for:

- one node and one mesh;
- material names, and relative texture paths inside `textures/`;
- single-sided materials;
- the triangle limit;
- the pivot;
- faces pointing outwards (positive enclosed volume).

It also records whether a ray straight down through the centre hits the toy (`covers_centre`). The
tests require that for the disc, so it can never become a ring.

`render_toy.py` also writes `out/glb/toy_<design>.glb`, a self-contained copy of each toy (any
textures embedded) for a three.js viewer. They are not committed: the `exports/` glTFs are the
source of truth.

## Rebuilding

From the repository root, with headless Blender as a Python module (`pip install bpy==5.3.0`) plus
numpy and Pillow:

```
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/export_toy.py [outroot] [--designs disc,chew_cone,...]   # a few seconds
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/tests/test_toy.py                                        # about 20 s
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/render_toy.py [OUT_DIR] --views glb
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/render_toy.py [OUT_DIR] --views closeup --designs disc,chew_cone,hotdog
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/render_toy.py [OUT_DIR] --views closeup --designs rubber_chicken,rope_knot
python3 art-source/blender/items/toy/render_toy.py [OUT_DIR] --views lineup
```

- `export_toy.py` prints `ALL OK` when every check passes.
- Two runs in fresh processes write byte-identical textures, exports and stats. The test rebuilds
  twice into a temporary folder and compares both runs with the committed files.
- The glTF exporter prints harmless `Draco` / `MeshOptimizer` library errors (neither is used).

**Render a few views per command.** In a container without a GPU, EEVEE runs on Mesa's lavapipe.
After several renders in one process it can crash with no error message, leaving the later sheets
missing, so split the work as shown above.

The renders read the approved lawn, road, paving, house and Labrador exports from
`art-source/blender/` by relative path. They take about 1–2 minutes per close-up sheet and 5
minutes for the line-ups, and they are not byte-reproducible.
